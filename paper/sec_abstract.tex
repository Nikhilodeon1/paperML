Parameters of a mechanistic simulator fitted to a person's wearable data are routinely read as physiology, and held-out
prediction is the usual evidence that this is safe.
We ask what the incremental area under the curve (iAUC) of postprandial continuous glucose monitoring, and the full trace,
determine about the insulin sensitivity and the gastric emptying and absorption rates of a differentiable Bergman-type
simulator, and whether held-out iAUC prediction would reveal what they do not.
We combine symbolic analysis, Fisher information, profile likelihood, a replica in which the model is true and a synthetic
study with known truths, on three cohorts (\resCohortCgmSubjects, \resCohortShanghaiSubjects{} and \resCohortHallSubjects{}
subjects).
We find that the iAUC carries little information about timing: the weakest Fisher direction is the exchange of the two gut rates,
an exact symmetry of the empty-gut model, and no profile interval for either rate is bounded.
The interval for insulin sensitivity is bounded for \resProfileIaucSIInteriorBoundedOne{} of
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorNOne{} subjects with an interior estimate (\resRealIaucSIBoundedAll{} of \resRealIaucN{} overall) and
for \resSeedsSIBounded\% of interior subjects when the model is exactly true.
On replicas with random truths the held-out iAUC error is \resPvpRandomZeroRatio{} times that of the true model while the rates
are off by a median root mean squared log error of \resPvpRandomZeroTiming, so prediction does not reveal the failure, although
held-out peak-time and trace error rise with it.
From the full trace, a combination of the two rates is bounded for \resPolishedBoundedTauTrace\% and
\resPolishedBoundedPTrace\% of real subjects, but on true-model replicas the bounded intervals contain the truth in only
\resReplicaCoordsCoverTauTrace\% and \resReplicaCoordsCoverPTrace\% of cases, and carbohydrate logging error shifts the
estimate while the interval does not widen.
For this model, these targets and these cohorts, a good fit is not evidence that the fitted parameters mean what their names say.
