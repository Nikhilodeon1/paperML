Parameters of mechanistic simulators fitted to wearable data are often read as physiology, and held-out prediction is the usual justification.
We ask what the incremental area under the glucose curve (iAUC) and the full continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) trace after a meal determine about insulin sensitivity ($\SI$), gastric emptying ($\ke$) and carbohydrate absorption ($\ka$) in a differentiable Bergman-type simulator.
We use three cohorts (\resCohortCgmSubjects, \resCohortShanghaiSubjects{} and \resCohortHallSubjects{} subjects) and simulated controls with known truth.
Under iAUC, no profile-likelihood interval for $\ke$ or $\ka$ is bounded at any window from $60$ to $180$\,min, and the interval for $\SI$ is bounded for \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOne\% of subjects with an interior estimate at the primary parameter box (\resSeedsSIBounded\% when the model is exactly true).
Held-out iAUC error does not reveal this. On random-truth replicas, fits whose gut rates are pinned to deliberately wrong box corners, with $\SI$ refitted, stay within \resNegZeroSlowRefitIauc{} to \resPvpRandomZeroRatio{} times the error of the true model, whereas held-out peak-time and trace errors do rise.
The full trace bounds a symmetric combination of the two rates, the sum $\tau_1$ and the product $p$ of their reciprocals, for \resPolishedBoundedTauTrace\% and \resPolishedBoundedPTrace\% of interior CGMacros subjects, and for \resExactDRealTauIntPct\% when the search is confined to the exactly feasible rate region.
Boundedness is not calibration, however.
On replicas, a $25\%$ carbohydrate logging error raises the bounded share from \resExactDZeroTauIntPct\% to \resExactDQuarterTauIntPct\% while nominal coverage falls from \resExactDZeroTauCovNom\% to \resExactDQuarterTauCovNom\%, and a meal-clustered sandwich correction restores it only in part.
Traces generated from a nonlinear Dalla Man model give bounded Bergman intervals for \resMisspecTauPct\% of subjects that cover the true mean appearance time in \resMisspecCovDm\% of them, although they preserve the order of subjects (Spearman $\rho=\resMisspecRhoDm$).
For these models, targets and cohorts, neither held-out accuracy nor a bounded profile shows that a fitted parameter measures physiology.
