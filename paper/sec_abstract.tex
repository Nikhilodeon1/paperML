Parameters of a mechanistic model fitted to wearable data are easily read as physiology.
We ask which parameters of a differentiable Bergman-type glucose--insulin simulator can actually be
recovered from free-living postprandial continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) when the fitted target is
the baseline-subtracted incremental area under the curve (iAUC), and whether held-out prediction would
tell us.
We combine the full Fisher information matrix, profile likelihood, a generic-rank test, symbolic
structural-identifiability analysis, a replica experiment in which the model is true, and held-out
prediction on three cohorts, under an analysis plan fixed in version control before the analyses were run.
Gastric emptying and carbohydrate absorption cannot be separated: their exchange is an exact symmetry of
the empty-gut model, the weakest Fisher direction lies along it in every subject of every cohort, and no
profile interval for either rate is bounded under iAUC at any parameter range we tried.
Insulin sensitivity is not reliably recovered either: among subjects whose estimate is interior, its
interval is bounded for only \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOne\% at the primary parameter range, and for
\resSeedsSIBounded\% when the model is exactly true.
What can be recovered is a combination of the two rates: the sum and the product of their reciprocals are
bounded in \resCoordsBoundedTauTrace\% and \resCoordsBoundedPTrace\% of subjects under the full glucose
trace, but in only \resCoordsBoundedTauCentroid\% and \resCoordsBoundedPCentroid\% when the area is
supplemented by its timing centroid.
None of this is visible to prediction.
Gradient fitting and exhaustive grid search differ in held-out iAUC error by
\resDiffGradThreeVsGridThree\ mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min ($95\%$ interval \resDiffGradThreeVsGridThreeLow\ to
\resDiffGradThreeVsGridThreeHigh) on a mean error of \resMaeGradThree, three fitted parameters match
insulin sensitivity alone, and a full-trace objective does not lower held-out iAUC error.
Of the \resCountGraded\ hypotheses with a pass-or-fail rule that we evaluated, \resCountMet\ were met and \resCountNotMet\ were not; we report all of them.
A good fit to postprandial CGM is therefore not evidence that the fitted physiological parameters mean what
their names say, and an identifiability analysis should accompany any digital twin whose parameters are
interpreted.
