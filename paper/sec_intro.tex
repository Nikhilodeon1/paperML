\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A glucose digital twin can tell a user that their insulin sensitivity is low or their gastric emptying is slow.
Such statements rest on parameters of a mechanistic simulator fitted to continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) records and meal logs, usually justified by held-out predictive accuracy.
Predictive fit, however, does not guarantee parameter identification.
When an observation constrains only a combination of parameters, the optimizer still returns individual point estimates, whose split reflects initialization and box bounds rather than physiology.

Identifiability analysis separates what the data constrain from what the estimator supplies \citep[see][for reviews]{cobelli1980parameter,miao2011identifiability,villaverde2016structural}.
Related ambiguities are classical in pharmacokinetics and clinical metabolic modeling, including flip-flop kinetics in oral absorption \citep{boxenbaum1998flipflop,yanez2011flipflop}, zero insulin sensitivity estimates in minimal-model fits \citep{pillonetto2002minimal}, and recent practical-identifiability studies on clinical oral glucose tolerance tests with plasma insulin \citep{erdos2024leveraging}.
Here we study the free-living regime in which consumer digital twins operate: self-logged meals, continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) without plasma insulin, and an end-to-end differentiable Bergman-type simulator \citep{kidger2021diffrax,bradbury2018jax} evaluated under both incremental area under the curve (iAUC) and full postprandial traces.

We ask which of insulin sensitivity $\SI$, gastric emptying $\ke$, and carbohydrate absorption $\ka$ are constrained by free-living CGM data, how identification depends on target, window, box, noise, and input error, and whether held-out prediction exposes poorly recovered parameters.
To separate target limitations from model misfit, we pair three public cohorts with a \emph{true-model replica}, a random-truth synthetic study, and a Dalla Man $\to$ Bergman misspecified-generator control.
Throughout, we distinguish three levels of evidence: (i) \emph{observed likelihood boundedness} (whether a profile interval closes inside the parameter domain), (ii) \emph{conditional simulation calibration} (whether intervals cover known truth under specified noise, input-error, and model assumptions), and (iii) \emph{real physiological identification} (whether fitted parameters measure human physiology, which observational CGM logs alone do not establish).

We make three main contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Observable-dependent identifiability and moment structure.} Moment analysis of the two-compartment gut explains why finite-window area weakly constrains the transit-time-preserving tangent direction, while an empty gut adds a discrete exchange symmetry (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). Empirically, zero profile intervals for $\ke$ or $\ka$ are bounded under iAUC across three cohorts, $60$--$180$\,min windows, and a nonlinear Dalla Man model (Sections~\ref{sec:timing} and~\ref{sec:dm}), whereas $\SI$ is bounded only for a box-dependent minority (\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOne\% of interior CGMacros subjects at the primary box) and identifies a lumped gain $\gamma p_3 \SI$ conditional on fixed constants (Section~\ref{sec:si}).
\item \textbf{Held-out prediction conceals severe parameter error.} Across CGMacros and Shanghai cross-validation, fitting $(\ke,\ka)$ matches fitting $\SI$ alone within $\pm 150$\,mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min. On random-truth replicas, both three-parameter fits and an adversarial control pinning $(\ke,\ka)$ to opposite box corners (with $\SI$ refitted) stay within \resNegZeroSlowRefitIauc--\resPvpRandomZeroRatio{} times the oracle iAUC floor, whereas held-out peak-time and full-trace errors expose the timing failure (Section~\ref{sec:prediction}).
\item \textbf{Identifiability diagnostics are fragile to input error and structural misspecification.} Full-trace profiles achieve high likelihood boundedness for symmetric combinations $(\tau_1, p)$ even when restricted strictly to the original feasible rate domain $\mathcal{D}$ (\resExactDRealTauIntPct\% of interior CGMacros subjects; Section~\ref{sec:more}). Yet strictly inside $\mathcal{D}$, carbohydrate logging error shifts the estimates upward and off the lower box wall, which coincides with more bounded profiles and much lower coverage (only partially repaired by meal-clustered sandwich scaling). Even with exact carbohydrates, generating traces from a nonlinear Dalla Man model yields \resMisspecTauPct\% bounded Bergman intervals with \resMisspecCovDm\% absolute coverage of true mean appearance times despite preserving cross-subject rank order (Section~\ref{sec:dm}).
\end{enumerate}
