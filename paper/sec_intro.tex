\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A glucose digital twin can tell a person that their insulin sensitivity is low or that their stomach empties
slowly. These statements are parameters of a simulator fitted to that person's continuous glucose monitoring
(CGM) record and meal log, and the usual evidence that they deserve trust is that the fitted simulator predicts
held-out data. Prediction can be good while the parameters are not determined. If two parameters enter the
observation only through a combination, the data fix the combination and leave the split open; the optimizer
returns a split anyway, and the split reflects its starting point and the parameter range.

Identifiability analysis separates what the data determine from what the fitting procedure supplies
\citep[see][for reviews]{cobelli1980parameter,miao2011identifiability,villaverde2016structural}. Related
ambiguities are old. In oral pharmacokinetics, flip-flop occurs when absorption is slower than elimination, so
that the terminal slope after extravascular dosing reflects absorption \citep{boxenbaum1998flipflop,
yanez2011flipflop}. In the minimal model of glucose kinetics, nonlinear least squares in the MINMOD software often
returns an insulin sensitivity of zero in subjects with type two diabetes \citep{pillonetto2002minimal}, and personalised
glucose--insulin models calibrated on an oral glucose tolerance test with CGM and plasma insulin have been
examined for practical identifiability \citep{erdos2024leveraging}. We study the case in which twins are
deployed: meals that people log themselves, no plasma insulin, a postprandial summary as the target, and a
simulator that is differentiable end to end \citep{kidger2021diffrax,bradbury2018jax}, so that Fisher information
and profile likelihoods are available for every person.

We take one widely used target, the baseline-subtracted incremental area under the glucose curve (iAUC) of a
meal, and a Bergman-type simulator with a two-compartment gut. Three questions follow. Which of insulin
sensitivity $\SI$, gastric emptying rate $\ke$ and carbohydrate absorption rate $\ka$ do the data determine? Is
the answer a property of the target, the optimizer, the noise or a wrong model? Would held-out prediction have
told us? We answer with the full Fisher information matrix and the Schur complement of its timing block,
profile-likelihood intervals with fixed definitions of bounded, one-sided and flat, a generic-rank test and
symbolic structural analysis. Two instruments that real data cannot supply carry the weight: a \emph{replica},
in which the model is true and the noise and the carbohydrate-logging error are the subject's own, and a
synthetic study with randomly drawn truths. We replicate on three cohorts, repeat the prediction comparison on a
second cohort and run the same questions on a second gut model. The analysis plan was fixed in version control
before the analyses and amended \resAmendmentCount\ times; Appendix~\ref{app:register} lists every amendment and
marks the hypotheses declared after results were seen.

Our contributions are three.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We show that an iAUC carries little information about the timing of glucose entry. Moments of the gut input
  explain why (Proposition~\ref{prop:moments}), and the Fisher information of three parameters has one direction
  that is essentially null. That direction is the exchange of the two gut rates, which we prove is an exact symmetry
  of the empty-gut cascade. It is the weakest direction in every subject of three cohorts, and no profile interval for
  either rate is bounded (Sections~\ref{sec:theory} and~\ref{sec:timing}).
\item We show with a replica and a synthetic study that the insulin-sensitivity interval is bounded for
  \resProfileIaucSIInteriorBoundedOne{} of \resProfileIaucSIInteriorNOne{} subjects with an interior estimate
  (\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOne\%, Wilson interval \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOneLow\ to
  \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOneHigh\%) at the primary box and for \resSeedsSIBounded\% when the model is exactly
  true. The figure depends on the box: \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracHalf\% at half and
  \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracTwo\% at twice the width. On one replica seed, cutting the noise to a quarter
  raises it from \resNoiseOneIaucSI\% to \resNoiseQuarterIaucSI\% (Section~\ref{sec:si}).
\item We show that held-out iAUC prediction does not reveal either failure: gradient fitting and grid search differ by
  \resDiffGradThreeVsGridThree{} mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min, and three parameters match insulin sensitivity alone within a
  margin of $150$, in two cohorts (Section~\ref{sec:prediction}). The margin is a stated choice, and the
  appendix reports $90$ and $300$ as well. On replicas with random truths the fit comes within
  \resPvpRandomZeroRatio{} and \resPvpRandomOneRatio{} times the error of the true model, with the rates off by a
  median root mean squared log error of \resPvpRandomZeroTiming{} and \resPvpRandomOneTiming{}, and across subjects
  the ratio is unrelated to the parameter error. A check on the peak time or the trace, which the fits were not
  trained on, would see it: their excess over the floor rises with the timing error.
\end{enumerate}
Three further results qualify these. The sum and product of the reciprocal rates look recoverable from the full
glucose trace on real data, but a true-model replica does not reproduce this at the estimated noise, and removing the
carbohydrate error from the replica shows that an input error can make such intervals both more often bounded and
wrong. The combination is recovered, with bounded and calibrated intervals, only in a replica with exact
carbohydrate and a quarter of the noise, and the low-noise undercoverage we first saw there came from reading the
profile off a coarse grid, which we show by computing it at the true value (Section~\ref{sec:more}). And a design that
breaks the exchange symmetry, by giving each meal a known residual gut content, raises the share of bounded
gut-rate intervals with the residue but does not make the rates identifiable at a tenth of the meal
(Section~\ref{sec:more}). A second gut model, the Dalla Man model, also leaves its gut parameters unbounded
(Section~\ref{sec:dm}). Table~\ref{tab:claims} maps each claim to its evidence and scope.

We do not claim a new fitting method, a clinical result or recovery of the combination of the two rates. The
claims hold for one model family, one target and three cohorts, and Section~\ref{sec:limits} says where they
