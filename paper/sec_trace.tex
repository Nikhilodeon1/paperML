\subsection{The full trace bounds \texorpdfstring{$\tau_1$ and $p$}{tau1 and p}, and logging error moves the intervals off the truth}
\label{sec:more}

The exchange symmetry leaves $\tau_1=1/\ke+1/\ka$ and $p=1/(\ke\ka)$ unchanged, so we fit the trace in $(\log\SI,\log\tau_1,\log p)$.
The search region is the smallest rectangle in these coordinates that contains the primary box.
Where $p>\tau_1^2/4$ no real rates exist, and the fit projects onto the equal-rate boundary while holding any profiled coordinate fixed.
Each estimate is refined by L-BFGS-B from several starting points, and every point of a $29$-point profile grid is solved to convergence with the same optimizer; where its line search fails, the point is solved again from two other starting points.
A coarser grid with a fixed number of optimizer steps, which we used first, overstates the excess likelihood at the truth and therefore understates coverage (Appendix~\ref{apx:numerics}).

On real CGMacros traces the interval for $\tau_1$ is bounded in \resCRealNTauBd{} of \resCRealNTauInt{} interior subjects, and the interval for $p$ in \resCRealNPBd{} of \resCRealNPInt{} (Table~\ref{tab:coords}).
By comparison, the iAUC with the excursion centroid bounded $\tau_1$ in only \resCoordsBoundedTauCentroid\% of interior subjects, although the centroid carries the first moment of the gut input (that run used the coarser protocol; Appendix~\ref{apx:more}).
These counts say that the likelihood closes inside the search region.
They do not say that the interval contains the right value, which only a simulation with known truth can test.

\paragraph{With exact inputs the intervals are close to nominal.}
In the replicas the truth is known and the fitted model is the one that generated the data.
We use two sets of truths: random draws through the box, with two seeds, and each subject's fitted values.
With exact carbohydrate and the subject's own estimated sensor noise, the $\tau_1$ interval contains the truth in \resCRandZeroTauCover{} of \resCRandZeroTauN{} subjects on random-truth seed~$0$ (seed~$1$: \resCRandZeroSeedOneTauCover), and the $p$ interval in \resCRandZeroPCover{} (seed~$1$: \resCRandZeroSeedOnePCover).
Many of these intervals are informative.
For $\tau_1$, \resCRandZeroTauNarrow{} (seed~$0$) and \resCRandZeroSeedOneTauNarrow{} (seed~$1$) are bounded and narrower than $0.5$ log units, and \resCRandZeroTauNarrowCovers{} and \resCRandZeroSeedOneTauNarrowCovers{} of those contain the truth (Table~\ref{tab:cvsweep}).
So the trace can identify the two combinations when the simulator and its inputs are right.

\paragraph{Logging error lowers coverage without widening the intervals.}
We then give the estimator carbohydrate values multiplied by lognormal noise with unit mean.
As its coefficient of variation (CV) grows, coverage falls on every truth set (Figure~\ref{fig:cvsweep}).
At a CV of $0.25$ the effect is visible for $\tau_1$ and small for $p$.
At $0.75$ most intervals for $\tau_1$ miss: on seed~$0$, \resCRandThreeQuarterTauCover{} of \resCRandThreeQuarterTauN{} contain the truth (seed~$1$: \resCRandThreeQuarterSeedOneTauCover).
The estimate moves toward slower absorption.
The median signed log error of $\tau_1$ on seed~$0$ rises from \resCRandZeroTauShift{} with exact carbohydrate to \resCRandQuarterTauShift{} at $0.25$ and \resCRandThreeQuarterTauShift{} at $0.75$, where it reaches \resCRandThreeQuarterSeedOneTauShift{} on seed~$1$.
The median interval width does not grow to match; it is \resCRandZeroTauWidth{} log units with exact carbohydrate and \resCRandThreeQuarterTauWidth{} at $0.75$.
At the larger error the typical shift therefore exceeds half the typical width.
The likelihood treats the logged load as exact, so the interval reflects sensor noise and has no term for the input error.
We have not established why the shift is toward slower absorption.

The order of subjects survives better than the coverage does.
On seed~$0$ the rank correlation between estimated and true $\tau_1$ is \resCRandZeroTauRankRho{} with exact carbohydrate, \resCRandQuarterTauRankRho{} at a CV of $0.25$, and \resCRandThreeQuarterTauRankRho{} at $0.75$, and on seed~$1$ it is \resCRandZeroSeedOneTauRankRho, \resCRandQuarterSeedOneTauRankRho, and \resCRandThreeQuarterSeedOneTauRankRho.
With fitted truths, which are bunched near one edge, it is lower even with exact inputs (\resCFitZeroTauRankRho).

\paragraph{A bounded interval is not a sign of better data.}
The number of bounded intervals responds to the same error in opposite directions.
With random truths it falls for $\tau_1$, from \resCRandZeroTauBd{} subjects with exact carbohydrate to \resCRandThreeQuarterTauBd{} at a CV of $0.75$ on seed~$0$.
With fitted truths it rises, from \resCFitZeroTauBd{} to \resCFitQuarterTauBd{} between exact carbohydrate and $0.25$.
The difference lies in where the truths are.
The fitted truths of $\tau_1$ sit at a median of \resCFitZeroTauTruthPosPct\% of the search range above its lower edge, and the random truths at \resCRandZeroTauTruthPosPct\%.
Near the edge, \resCFitZeroTauOpenLow{} intervals are open on the lower side with exact carbohydrate.
The shift toward larger $\tau_1$ carries estimates away from that edge, and only \resCFitQuarterTauOpenLow{} remain open below at $0.25$.
In the middle of the range the same shift moves estimates toward the upper edge, where the number of intervals open above grows from \resCRandZeroTauOpenHigh{} with exact carbohydrate to \resCRandThreeQuarterTauOpenHigh{} at a CV of $0.75$ on seed~$0$.
Hence the bounded count on real data cannot be turned into a statement about the size of the logging error, and a noisier input can produce more bounded intervals with fewer correct ones.

\paragraph{Breaking the symmetry by design.}
If the exchange symmetry alone kept $\ke$ and $\ka$ unbounded, a known residue in the gut should bound them (Proposition~\ref{prop:breaking}).
We added a residue of $10\%$ of each meal, equal in the truth and in the fit, to a random-truth replica with exact carbohydrate.
The interval is then bounded for $\ke$ in \resResTenKeBoundedCount{} of \resResTenKeBoundedN{} interior subjects and for $\ka$ in \resResTenKaBoundedCount{} of \resResTenKaBoundedN.
This control ran only under the coarser protocol (Appendix~\ref{apx:phase13}), so we read it as a direction and not as a calibrated count: removing the symmetry helps a few subjects, and most rates stay unbounded at this noise level.
