\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Hypotheses H1 to H10 were fixed in version control before the analyses. H11 and H12 were declared before theirs, and
H13 to H26 after earlier results had been seen; those are marked post hoc. Fractions carry Wilson intervals and paired
differences carry subject-level bootstrap intervals. Appendix~\ref{app:results} gives the tables and figures omitted
here, and Appendix~\ref{app:scorecard} the status of every hypothesis.

\subsection{An iAUC carries little information about $\ke$ and $\ka$}
\label{sec:timing}\label{sec:structural}

\paragraph{What the area carries.}
At the primary box the Fisher information of $(\log\SI,\log\ke,\log\ka)$ under iAUC has median eigenvalues
\resEigMedianOne, \resEigMedianTwo{} and \resEigMedianThree. One direction is essentially null and a second is weak,
so the area carries between one and two effective dimensions of the three parameters. The coordinates in which the
exchange leaves the model unchanged, $\tau_1=1/\ke+1/\ka$ and $p=1/(\ke\ka)$, are not bounded either: under iAUC the
interval for $\tau_1$ is bounded in \resCoordsBoundedTauIauc\% and that for $p$ in \resCoordsBoundedPIauc\% of subjects
interior for them. The exchange of the two rates is therefore not the whole of the failure. It is the exact
symmetry that Proposition~\ref{prop:exchange} proves in the empty-gut model, and the rest of this section shows that it
is also the weakest direction of the information matrix.

\paragraph{Structure.}
Symbolic analysis with StructuralIdentifiability.jl (Appendix~\ref{app:structural}) finds the rates globally
identifiable when the intestinal content is a known generic value, and only locally identifiable when it is
unconstrained. In canonical coordinates the sum $\ke+\ka$ and the product $\ke\ka$ are globally identifiable
(Table~\ref{tab:structural}). The plan said to drop the exchange framing if the rates were globally identifiable, and
the planned formulation triggers that rule. H1 is therefore reported as formulation-dependent, not as met. We keep the
framing because the empty-gut model that the simulator runs is exactly symmetric (Proposition~\ref{prop:exchange}):
exchanging the rates changes simulated glucose by at most $\resGutSweepMaxZero$\,mg\,dL$^{-1}$. The stacked
sensitivity matrix has generic rank three in \resRankFullIauc{} of \resRankDrawsIauc{} random draws for iAUC and in
every draw for the richer observables. Under iAUC the parameters are locally identifiable but ill-conditioned.

\paragraph{Estimates, Fisher information and profiles.}
At the primary box the maximum-likelihood estimates of $\ke$ and $\ka$ sit on a bound in \resPinnedFracKeOne\% and
\resPinnedFracKaOne\% of the \resSubjectsOne{} CGMacros subjects. The optimizer is not the cause. The Adam estimate
exceeds the best of six L-BFGS runs by at most \resOptGapMaxOne{} in negative log-likelihood, below $\delta$.

The weakest eigenvector of the timing block of the Fisher information lies along the exchange direction, with a median
absolute cosine of \resCosineIaucOne{} under iAUC (H4, planned, \resVerdictHFour). It does so in every subject of all
three cohorts (Figure~\ref{fig:symmetry}b; H15, post hoc, \resVerdictHFifteen). No profile interval for $\ke$ or $\ka$
is bounded under iAUC: \resProfileIaucKeOne\% and \resProfileIaucKaOne\% of subjects at the primary box, with upper
Wilson limits of \resProfileIaucKeOneHigh\% and \resProfileIaucKaOneHigh\%. The same holds at half and twice the box and
with the grid extended to four times the upper bound (timing clause of H5, planned, met). The Shanghai cohort
(\resRepShanghaiOneN{} subjects) and the cohort with standardized meals (\resRepHallOneN{} subjects) agree. Most of
their estimates sit on a bound at the primary box, so the replication rests on the Fisher alignment and the flat
profiles. Part of the pinning is an area deficit. Subjects whose rates sit on the upper bound have much larger
responses and are under-predicted by \resBiasSignedKe{} mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min on average, so the model cannot deliver
their area at any admissible timing.

\subsection{$\SI$ is recoverable for some subjects and not others, and noise explains only part of why}
\label{sec:si}

\paragraph{Real data.}
The plan required the $\SI$ interval to be bounded in at least $80\%$ of subjects interior for $\SI$ (H5, planned).
It is bounded in \resProfileIaucSIInteriorBoundedOne{} of \resProfileIaucSIInteriorNOne{}
(\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOne\%, Wilson interval \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOneLow\ to
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOneHigh\%) at the primary box, in \resProfileIaucSIInteriorBoundedHalf{} of
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorNHalf{} (\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracHalf\%, \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracHalfLow\ to
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracHalfHigh\%) at half the box and in \resProfileIaucSIInteriorBoundedTwo{} of
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorNTwo{} (\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracTwo\%, \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracTwoLow\ to
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracTwoHigh\%) at twice the box. The clause fails at every width (\resVerdictHFive).
A wider box lets more profiles close, but it also changes which subjects count as interior, so the three
denominators differ. The fractions describe what the data support within a stated range, not a property of the
subject. Most intervals are one-sided, and the picture holds at the $90\%$ level (\resLevelNinetySIInteriorOne\%) and
the $99\%$ level (\resLevelNinetyNineSIInteriorOne\%).

\paragraph{When the model is true.}
The replica gives the same answer. The model is exactly true, the noise is the subject's own and the carbohydrate
logging error is $25\%$. On seed zero the interval is bounded in \resReplicaSIBoundedCount{} of
\resReplicaSIInterior{} interior subjects, or \resReplicaSIBounded\% (H13, post hoc, \resVerdictHThirteen). Pooled over
five seeds it is bounded in \resSeedsSIBounded\% (\resSeedsSIBoundedCount{} of \resSeedsSIInterior{}; the seeds reuse
the same subjects and meal schedules, so the Wilson interval treats them as more independent than they are), with
\resSeedsRangeLow--\resSeedsRangeHigh\% across seeds and no seed reaching the $50\%$ of the rule (H17, post hoc,
\resVerdictHSeventeen). The pooled bounded intervals are calibrated, with coverage \resSeedsCoverSI\%. The interval for
$\ke$ or $\ka$ is never bounded.

With true parameters drawn across the box (the synthetic study, \resSynthN{} subjects, post hoc), the $\SI$ interval is
bounded for \resSynthTercileSILow\%, \resSynthTercileSIMid\% and \resSynthTercileSIHigh\% of interior subjects in the
lower, middle and upper third of the true value (Figure~\ref{fig:replica}b), with coverage \resSynthCoverSI\%. Again no
interval for $\ke$ or $\ka$ is bounded: the estimates return a rate on a bound even when the truth lies inside the box.

\paragraph{How much is noise.}
The replica noise is estimated from real residuals, which include model misfit, so it is an upper bound on the sensor
noise. Scaling it to one half and one quarter raises the bounded fraction of $\SI$ under iAUC from
\resNoiseOneIaucSI\% to \resNoiseHalfIaucSI\% and \resNoiseQuarterIaucSI\% (seed zero throughout, so the first figure
is the seed-zero \resReplicaSIBounded\% and not the pooled \resSeedsSIBounded\%; H24, post hoc). That is still
below the $80\%$ of the plan. The weakness is partly a property of the noise level and partly of the target.
Carbohydrate logging error does not cause it, since \resExactOneIaucSI\% are bounded with exact carbohydrate. Two
heteroskedastic variants of the likelihood do not bound $\SI$ more often either (\resHeteroRoot\% and \resHeteroProp\%
against \resHeteroBaseline\%; H16, exploratory; Appendix~\ref{apx:si}). A systematic error in the carbohydrate log is
absorbed by $\SI$. Scaling every logged carbohydrate by $0.75$ and $1.33$ moves the median estimate by a log ratio of
\resCarbScaleLowLogRatio{} and \resCarbScaleHighLogRatio{}, close to the \resCarbScaleLowExpected{} and
\resCarbScaleHighExpected{} of exact absorption (H16, exploratory).

\subsection{Held-out iAUC prediction does not reveal the failures}
\label{sec:prediction}

Gradient fitting and exhaustive grid search of the same loss differ in held-out iAUC error by
\resDiffGradThreeVsGridThree{} mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min ($95\%$ interval \resDiffGradThreeVsGridThreeLow{} to
\resDiffGradThreeVsGridThreeHigh) with three parameters, on a mean error of \resMaeGradThree{}. This is CGMacros,
$\resCohortCgmSubjects$ subjects, five repeats of five-fold cross-validation (Table~\ref{tab:prediction}). The $90\%$
intervals lie inside the margins of $150$ and $90$ (H7, planned, \resVerdictHSeven). Three parameters and $\SI$ alone
differ by \resDiffGradThreeVsGradOne{} (\resDiffGradThreeVsGradOneLow{} to \resDiffGradThreeVsGradOneHigh), which is
equivalent at the same margins (H8, planned, \resVerdictHEight). The timing parameters add less to held-out iAUC error
than the margin, although the interval excludes zero: the gain is small and not nil. A full-trace objective does not lower the error; it is \resDiffGradThreeTraceVsGradThree{} worse (H9,
planned, \resVerdictHNine), although it improves the quantities it targets.

The gradient fit beats the personal mean on CGMacros (\resDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMean{},
\resDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMeanLow{} to \resDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMeanHigh, on a personal-mean error of
\resMaePersonalMean), a random forest and a simulation-based-inference estimate. We call these
differences, not equivalences. On Shanghai the interval against the personal mean includes zero
(\resShCvDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMean{}, \resShCvDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMeanLow{} to
\resShCvDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMeanHigh), so the twin is a modest gain over a subject's own average there at best. In the Shanghai cohort (\resShCvSubjects{} subjects) the two comparisons give
\resShCvDiffGradThreeVsGridThree{} (\resShCvDiffGradThreeVsGridThreeLow{} to \resShCvDiffGradThreeVsGridThreeHigh) and
\resShCvDiffGradThreeVsGradOne{} (\resShCvDiffGradThreeVsGradOneLow{} to \resShCvDiffGradThreeVsGradOneHigh), inside the
margin of $150$, which is \resShCvMarginShare\% of the personal-mean error (H20, post hoc, \resVerdictHTwenty). There
the three-parameter fit beats the one-parameter fit by an interval that excludes zero. The timing parameters carry a
small amount of predictive information, and we do not say that they carry none.

Prediction of other quantities points the same way, and more sharply. On CGMacros the three-parameter fit predicts the
time of the peak with an error of \resPeakGradThree{} minutes and the whole trace with a root mean squared error of
\resTraceRmseGradThree{} mg\,dL$^{-1}$, against \resPeakGradOne{} minutes and \resTraceRmseGradOne{} for the fit of $\SI$
alone with the population timing held fixed (Table~\ref{tab:prediction}). Fitting the two timing parameters makes these
held-out predictions worse, which is consistent with fitted values that reflect the optimizer's choice more than the
subject's physiology. Our claim that prediction does not reveal the failures is therefore limited to the target the model
was fitted to, the iAUC. Prediction of the peak time and the trace does register a cost of the fitted timing parameters,
and we recommend reporting such checks beside the target-matched one.

\subsection{Whether a combination of the rates is recoverable is not established}
\label{sec:more}

The exchange symmetry leaves a combination of the rates free, $\tau_1=1/\ke+1/\ka$ and $p=1/(\ke\ka)$. The full trace
carries far more timing information than the area (Appendix~\ref{apx:more}). Fitting in coordinates
$(\log\SI,\log\tau_1,\log p)$, the interval for $\tau_1$ is bounded in \resCoordsBoundedTauIauc\%,
\resCoordsBoundedTauCentroid\% and \resCoordsBoundedTauTrace\% of subjects interior for it under iAUC, iAUC with the
centroid and the trace. For $p$ the figures are \resCoordsBoundedPIauc\%, \resCoordsBoundedPCentroid\% and
\resCoordsBoundedPTrace\% (H11, planned, \resVerdictHEleven). The controls we ran do not support reading this as
identifiability. The trace fits are not likelihood optima for \resPolishNotWithinTrace\% of subjects (H21, post hoc,
\resVerdictHTwentyOne). The like-for-like comparison uses polished estimates on both sides. On real data the polished
trace fits bound $\tau_1$ in \resPolishedBoundedTauTraceCount{} of \resPolishedBoundedTauTraceN{}
(\resPolishedBoundedTauTrace\%, \resPolishedBoundedTauTraceLow\ to \resPolishedBoundedTauTraceHigh\%) and $p$ in
\resPolishedBoundedPTraceCount{} of \resPolishedBoundedPTraceN{} (\resPolishedBoundedPTrace\%,
\resPolishedBoundedPTraceLow\ to \resPolishedBoundedPTraceHigh\%) of the subjects interior for them. On a true-model
replica they bound $\tau_1$ in \resReplicaCoordsTauTraceCount{} of \resReplicaCoordsTauTraceN{}
(\resReplicaCoordsTauTrace\%, \resReplicaCoordsTauTraceLow\ to \resReplicaCoordsTauTraceHigh\%) and $p$ in
\resReplicaCoordsPTraceCount{} of \resReplicaCoordsPTraceN{} (\resReplicaCoordsPTrace\%,
\resReplicaCoordsPTraceLow\ to \resReplicaCoordsPTraceHigh\%) (H18, post hoc, \resVerdictHEighteen). The Wilson intervals for
$\tau_1$ overlap, so for $\tau_1$ the difference in how often the interval is bounded is not established; for $p$ they
do not overlap. The coverage is the stronger evidence. The bounded replica
intervals contain the truth in only \resReplicaCoordsCoverTauTrace\% and \resReplicaCoordsCoverPTrace\%. A cutoff
calibrated on the replica lowers the real-data fractions to \resRecalRealCalibratedTau\% and
\resRecalRealCalibratedP\% (H23, post hoc, \resVerdictHTwentyThree). Reducing the replica noise does not restore
coverage (H24, post hoc, \resVerdictHTwentyFour), but removing the carbohydrate logging error does. With exact
carbohydrate the nominal interval covers the truth for \resExactOneCoverTau\% and \resExactOneCoverP\% of subjects (H25,
post hoc, \resVerdictHTwentyFive), the empirical cutoff is \resExactOneCutoffTau{} against the nominal
\resDeltaThreshold, and the trace bounds $\tau_1$ and $p$ in only \resExactOneTraceBoundedTau\% and
\resExactOneTraceBoundedP\%. An input error therefore makes the trace intervals more often bounded and less often
right. The real-data fractions are consistent with the same mechanism, but we cannot show that it is the one at work,
and a second source of undercoverage at low noise is unidentified. We do not claim that the combination is
recoverable. Appendix~\ref{apx:more} gives the trail.

\subsection{A second model class}
\label{sec:dm}

The Dalla Man meal model \citep{dallaman2007}, in our implementation, has a three-compartment gut with a nonlinear
emptying law, so it inherits no exact exchange symmetry. We fitted it by maximum likelihood to the same iAUC data of the
same \resDmN{} subjects (H22, post hoc, \resVerdictHTwentyTwo). Its Fisher information has median effective rank
\resDmRankMedian{} of six parameters. The profile interval is bounded for $k_{abs}$, $k_{max}$ and $k_{min}$ in
\resDmBoundedKabs\%, \resDmBoundedKmax\% and \resDmBoundedKmin\% of subjects, and the estimates sit on a bound of their
range in \resDmPinnedKabs, \resDmPinnedKmax{} and \resDmPinnedKmin{} subjects. The claim this supports is narrow. Under
the iAUC target, in two models that differ in the structure of the gut, the gut parameters are not recovered from these
subjects. We ran no replica for this model, so we cannot say how much of its non-identification belongs to the target
and how much to misfit.

Two further checks are in Appendix~\ref{app:results}. The gradient diagnostic ranks the three parameters as the profile
likelihood does (H6, planned with a post hoc synthetic clause, \resVerdictHSix) but orders the two timing rates
unstably across initializations (H10, planned, \resVerdictHTen). An external check against HbA1c shows the correlation
of an amortized estimate largely disappearing once fasting glucose is partialled out.
