\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Fractions carry Wilson intervals and paired differences carry subject-level bootstrap intervals. Table~\ref{tab:register}
marks which analyses were planned and which were added later; Appendix~\ref{app:results} holds the tables and figures
omitted here.

\subsection{An iAUC carries little information about $\ke$ and $\ka$}
\label{sec:timing}\label{sec:structural}

At the primary box we find that the Fisher information of $(\log\SI,\log\ke,\log\ka)$ under iAUC has median eigenvalues
\resEigMedianOne, \resEigMedianTwo{} and \resEigMedianThree. One direction is essentially null and a second is weak, so
the area carries between one and two effective dimensions of the three. The weakest eigenvector of the timing block lies
along the exchange direction, with a median absolute cosine of \resCosineIaucOne{} against the planned threshold of
$0.8$, and it does so in every subject of all three cohorts (Figure~\ref{fig:symmetry}b). We find no bounded profile interval for $\ke$
or $\ka$: the fractions are \resProfileIaucKeOne\% and \resProfileIaucKaOne\% of the \resSubjectsOne{}
CGMacros subjects at the primary box, with upper Wilson limits of \resProfileIaucKeOneHigh\% and
\resProfileIaucKaOneHigh\%. The same holds at half and twice the box and with the grid extended to four times the upper
bound.

The Shanghai (\resRepShanghaiOneN{} subjects) and standardized-meal (\resRepHallOneN{} subjects) cohorts agree, but most
of their estimates sit on a bound at the primary box. Only \resRepShanghaiOneInteriorKe{} and \resRepShanghaiOneInteriorKa{}
Shanghai subjects and \resRepHallOneInteriorKe{} and \resRepHallOneInteriorKa{} subjects of the third cohort are interior
for $\ke$ and $\ka$, so the replication there rests on the Fisher alignment and the flat profiles, not on interior
fractions. The optimizer is not the cause of the pinning: the Adam estimate exceeds the best of six L-BFGS runs by at
most \resOptGapMaxOne{} in negative log-likelihood, below the interval criterion $\delta$. Part of the pinning is an area deficit, which Appendix~\ref{apx:timing} describes.

Symbolic analysis with StructuralIdentifiability.jl (Appendix~\ref{app:structural}) gives an answer that depends on the
intestinal content at the start of a meal. With a known generic nonzero content the rates are globally identifiable, which
is the formulation the plan specified. With the content unconstrained, which includes the empty gut that the simulator
assumes, only the unordered pair is determined: $\ke+\ka$ and $\ke\ka$ are globally identifiable and the rates themselves
locally. We therefore record the planned symbolic test as formulation-dependent. Proposition~\ref{prop:exchange}
gives the empty-gut case exactly, and exchanging the rates changes simulated glucose by at most $\resGutSweepMaxZero$\,mg\,dL$^{-1}$.
The stacked sensitivity matrix has generic rank three in \resRankFullIauc{} of \resRankDrawsIauc{} random draws, so
under iAUC the parameters are locally identifiable but ill-conditioned.

\subsection{$\SI$ is recoverable for some subjects and not others}
\label{sec:si}

\paragraph{Real data.}
At the primary box the interval for $\SI$ is bounded in \resProfileIaucSIInteriorBoundedOne{} of the
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorNOne{} subjects with an interior estimate (\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOne\%, Wilson interval
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOneLow\ to \resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracOneHigh\%), which is \resRealIaucSIBoundedAll{} of
\resRealIaucN{} subjects overall. At half the box we find \resProfileIaucSIInteriorBoundedHalf{} of
\resProfileIaucSIInteriorNHalf{} and at twice the box \resProfileIaucSIInteriorBoundedTwo{} of \resProfileIaucSIInteriorNTwo{}
(\resProfileIaucSIInteriorFracTwo\%). A wider box lets more profiles close but also changes which subjects count as interior, so
the denominators differ and the fractions describe what the data support within a stated range, not a property of the subject.
The plan asked for at least $80\%$ at every box width, and no width reaches it.

\paragraph{When the model is true.}
In the replica we make the model exactly true, give each subject their own noise and add a carbohydrate logging error of $25\%$.
The interval for $\SI$ is bounded in \resSeedsSIBounded\% of interior subjects pooled over five seeds
(\resSeedsSIBoundedCount{} of \resSeedsSIInterior{}; \resClustSILow\ to \resClustSIHigh\% when we resample subjects, since the
seeds reuse them), with coverage \resSeedsCoverSI\%, and the interval for $\ke$ or $\ka$ is never bounded. With true parameters
drawn across the box (the synthetic study, \resSynthN{} subjects) the $\SI$ interval is bounded for \resSynthTercileSILow\%,
\resSynthTercileSIMid\% and \resSynthTercileSIHigh\% of interior subjects in the lower, middle and upper third of the true value
(Figure~\ref{fig:replica}b), with coverage \resSynthCoverSI\%.

We estimate the replica noise from real residuals, which include model misfit, so it overstates the sensor noise. Scaling it to
one half and one quarter raises the bounded fraction on seed zero from \resNoiseOneIaucSI\% to \resNoiseHalfIaucSI\% and
\resNoiseQuarterIaucSI\%, still below the planned $80\%$, so we read the weakness as partly a property of the noise level and
partly of the target. Carbohydrate logging error does not cause it (\resExactOneIaucSI\% are bounded with exact carbohydrate),
and heteroskedastic variants of the likelihood do not help (Appendix~\ref{apx:si}). A systematic error in the carbohydrate log is absorbed by $\SI$ (Appendix~\ref{apx:si}). The median signed log error of $\hat\SI$ on the unperturbed replica is
\resPertBaseBias{} (\resPertBaseInteriorBias{} among interior estimates). Logging error is not its source: with exact
carbohydrate the median is \resExactSIBias, with \resExactSIPinned{} of \resExactSIN{} estimates on a bound.

\paragraph{A wrong fixed parameter.}
Two parameters of the glucose equations, $\gamma$ (insulin secretion) and $p_3$ (the gain of insulin action), are fixed at
population values in every fit. The glucose trace depends on them and on $\SI$ only through the product $\gamma\,p_3\,\SI$, so
scaling $\gamma$ or $p_3$ is the same as scaling $\SI$: multiplying either by $1.3$ or $0.7$ in the truth of the replica gives
the same estimate of $\SI$ in \resPertSameUp{} of \resPertSameUpN{} and \resPertSameDown{} of \resPertSameDownN{} subjects. We
therefore read $\SI$ as the scale of insulin action. A wrong fixed parameter is absorbed one for one only while the estimate stays
inside its box. At $0.7$, \resPertGammaDownPinned{} estimates end on a bound against \resPertBasePinned{} on the unperturbed
replica and the nominal interval covers the truth for \resPertGammaDownSICover\% of subjects, while the share of bounded
intervals (\resPertGammaDownSIBounded\% against \resPertBaseSIBounded\%) gives no signal.

\subsection{Held-out iAUC prediction does not reveal the failures}
\label{sec:prediction}

Gradient fitting and exhaustive grid search of the same loss differ in held-out iAUC error by
\resDiffGradThreeVsGridThree{} mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min ($95\%$ interval \resDiffGradThreeVsGridThreeLow{} to
\resDiffGradThreeVsGridThreeHigh) with three parameters, on a mean error of \resMaeGradThree{} (CGMacros, five repeats of
five-fold cross-validation, Table~\ref{tab:prediction}). Three parameters and $\SI$ alone differ by
\resDiffGradThreeVsGradOne{} (\resDiffGradThreeVsGradOneLow{} to \resDiffGradThreeVsGradOneHigh). Both $90\%$ intervals lie
inside the equivalence margin of $150$ mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min, so the timing parameters add less than a stated margin to held-out
iAUC error; the interval excludes zero, so the gain is small and not nil. Folds that respect time give the same picture, and
so does the Shanghai cohort, where the twin is at best a modest gain over a subject's own average (Appendix~\ref{apx:prediction}).
We find that the gradient fit beats the personal mean on CGMacros (\resDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMean{},
\resDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMeanLow{} to \resDiffGradThreeVsPersonalMeanHigh, on a personal-mean error of \resMaePersonalMean), a
random forest and a simulation-based-inference estimate, as differences and not equivalences.

Other targets point the same way, more sharply. On CGMacros the three-parameter fit predicts the time of the peak with an error
of \resPeakGradThree{} minutes and the whole trace with a root mean squared error of \resTraceRmseGradThree{} mg\,dL$^{-1}$,
against \resPeakGradOne{} minutes and \resTraceRmseGradOne{} for the fit of $\SI$ alone with the timing held at population values.
Fitting the two timing parameters from the iAUC makes these held-out predictions worse, which is what fitted values that reflect
the optimizer's choice more than the subject's physiology would do. Our claim that prediction does not reveal the failures is
therefore limited to the target the model was fitted to.

\paragraph{Held-out error against parameter error.}
A replica knows the truth, so we can measure the claim. For each subject we compare the held-out error of the three-parameter fit
with the error of the model at the true parameters on the same meals and the same logged carbohydrate (the floor). On the two
replicas with truths drawn at random across the box the median ratio of fit to floor is \resPvpRandomZeroRatio{} and
\resPvpRandomOneRatio{}, while the median root mean squared log error of $\ke$ and $\ka$ is \resPvpRandomZeroTiming{} and
\resPvpRandomOneTiming{}: the fit comes within a few percent of the true model's error with rates that are far from the truth,
and across subjects the ratio is unrelated to the timing error (Table~\ref{tab:floor}; Figure~\ref{fig:pvp} in
Appendix~\ref{apx:prediction} adds the replica whose truths are the fitted values, ratio \resPvpFitTruthRatio{}, against
\resPvpFitTruthMean{} for the personal mean). Fits scored on other targets can see the error. The held-out peak-time and trace
errors of the same fits exceed their floors, and the excess rises with the timing error, while the fit of $\SI$ alone with the
timing held at population values stays close to the floor. A check on the peak time or the trace would therefore see the failure
that the iAUC check hides. This is a statement about replicas, where the floor is observable; on real data it is not.

\subsection{The combination of the rates from the full trace}
\label{sec:more}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The exchange symmetry leaves $\tau_1$ and $p$ free, and the full trace carries far more timing information than the area
(Appendix~\ref{apx:more}). We fit in the coordinates $(\log\SI,\log\tau_1,\log p)$ and polish each estimate by the best of
several L-BFGS-B runs, because the fixed-step Adam fits are not likelihood optima for \resPolishNotWithinTrace\% of subjects
under the trace. Profiles are read on a grid of fifteen points with a fixed-step inner solver; a converged solver, a longer inner
run and a grid of twenty-nine points change no profile class on the real data or on the carbohydrate-error replicas
(Appendix~\ref{apx:numerics}) and matter only for the narrow profiles of the low-noise replica.

\paragraph{Real data and the true-model replica.}
Table~\ref{tab:coords} gives, for each target, the share of subjects interior for the coordinate whose interval is bounded.
The centroid and the trace raise the shares above the iAUC, and with polished estimates the trace bounds $\tau_1$ in
\resPolishedBoundedTauTraceCount{} of \resPolishedBoundedTauTraceN{} subjects  and $p$ in \resPolishedBoundedPTraceCount{} of \resPolishedBoundedPTraceN{}. With thirty random starts in place of five we find the polished
likelihood within the cutoff for \resGlobalWithinCount{} of \resGlobalN{} subjects, so the polish is a global optimum by that
check. If these shares meant identifiability, a replica in which the model is true should reproduce them. At the estimated
noise it does not: the shares are lower, and the bounded replica intervals contain the truth in only
\resReplicaCoordsCoverTauTrace\% and \resReplicaCoordsCoverPTrace\% of cases.

\paragraph{Carbohydrate logging error.}
When we remove the carbohydrate logging error, coverage returns. With exact carbohydrate at the estimated noise the nominal
interval contains the truth for \resExactOneCoverTau\% and \resExactOneCoverP\% of subjects and the trace bounds $\tau_1$
and $p$ in only \resExactOneTraceBoundedTau\% and \resExactOneTraceBoundedP\%. An input error therefore makes the trace
intervals more often bounded and less often right, and Table~\ref{tab:cvsweep} shows that the effect grows with the size of
the error. The real-data fractions are reached on the replica for $\tau_1$ at a logging error of $0.5$ to $0.75$ and are
above every replica value for $p$, so the real-data pattern is what a larger input error than we assumed would produce. We
cannot show that it is the cause, since a mechanism outside the replica would look the same. The pattern replicates on two further replica seeds and on a replica built from the \resShanghaiN{} Shanghai subjects (Appendix~\ref{apx:phase13}).

\paragraph{Why the error does not widen the interval.}
The likelihood treats the logged carbohydrate as exact, and the fit absorbs part of an input error in the parameters, so the
residual barely grows and the interval does not widen. The median width of the bounded $\tau_1$ intervals stays near
\resCvWidthZeroTau{} in log units while the median error of the estimate rises from \resCvErrorZeroTau{} to
\resCvErrorThreeQuarterTau{} (Table~\ref{tab:cvsweep}), so the ratio of error to half-width, which is the loss of coverage,
goes from \resCvZZeroTau{} to \resCvZThreeQuarterTau{} for $\tau_1$ and from \resCvZZeroP{} to \resCvZThreeQuarterP{} for $p$:
the estimate moves and the interval stays. Re-estimating the noise standard deviation and autocorrelation at the polished
estimate leaves the coverage where it was (\resFinalQuarterTauCover\% and \resFinalQuarterPCover\% at $0.25$,
\resFinalThreeQuarterTauCover\% and \resFinalThreeQuarterPCover\% at $0.75$).

One account of the narrow intervals is arithmetic. A logged load that is the true load times a unit-mean multiplier has second
moment $1+\mathrm{CV}^2$ times the squared true load, so sensitivities, and with them the Fisher information measured against
an unchanged noise level, could grow with the error. We measured it. The median squared load ratio is \resCurvQuarterLoad{} at
$0.25$ and \resCurvThreeQuarterLoad{} at $0.75$, but the information about $(\tau_1,p)$ does not grow: the smaller eigenvalue of
its block after profiling out $\SI$ has median \resCurvZeroSchurLow{} at exact carbohydrate, \resCurvQuarterSchurLow{} at $0.25$
and \resCurvThreeQuarterSchurLow{} at $0.75$, because the noise standard deviation estimated at the fit rises from
\resCurvZeroSigma{} to \resCurvThreeQuarterSigma. Local curvature therefore explains neither the narrow intervals nor the rise of
the bounded share, which has no mechanism we can show: the profiles are not steeper over the box (median maximum rise
\resCvRiseZeroTau{} at exact carbohydrate, \resCvRiseThreeQuarterTau{} at $0.75$ for $\tau_1$) and the number of estimates on a
bound barely changes, yet \resAllZeroBoundedTau{} of \resAllZeroN{} $\tau_1$ intervals are bounded at exact carbohydrate and
\resAllThreeQuarterBoundedTau{} at $0.75$. A cutoff that covers the truth and a systematic per-subject error, two further tests of
the account, are in Appendix~\ref{apx:phase13}.

\paragraph{When the combination is recovered.}
We find one setting in which the trace returns intervals for $\tau_1$ and $p$ that are both often bounded and calibrated: the
replica with exact carbohydrate and a quarter of the noise, read on the finer grid with the converged solver. There the nominal
interval contains the truth for \resConvFineTauCover\% and \resConvFinePCover\% of subjects, the detector bounds $\tau_1$ and $p$
in \resConvFineTauBounded\% and \resConvFinePBounded\% of interior subjects, and the bounded intervals contain the truth in
\resConvFineTauCovbd\% and \resConvFinePCovbd\%. Read off the coarse grid the same profiles undercover
(\resInnerQuarterFiveHundredCoverTau\% and \resInnerQuarterFiveHundredCoverP\%), because linear interpolation across a grid that is
wide relative to a narrow profile overstates the excess at the truth (Appendix~\ref{apx:numerics}). The detector can therefore
identify the combination when the data and the inputs are good enough. The real data are not that setting: the real bounded
fractions of \resPolishedBoundedTauTrace\% and \resPolishedBoundedPTrace\% are consistent with a larger input error or a smaller
noise than we assumed and cannot be assigned to either.

\paragraph{Breaking the symmetry by design.}
If the exchange symmetry were the only reason the gut rates are not bounded, a design that breaks it should bound them. We gave
every meal of a random-truth replica with exact carbohydrate a known residual intestinal content, equal in the truth and in the
fit so that the model stays exactly true. At a content of $10\%$ of the meal, $\ke$ and $\ka$ are bounded in
\resResTenKeBoundedCount{} of \resResTenKeBoundedN{} and \resResTenKaBoundedCount{} of \resResTenKaBoundedN{} interior subjects
(\resResTenKeBounded\% and \resResTenKaBounded\%) and covered for \resResTenKeCover\% and \resResTenKaCover\%, against the planned
half bounded and ninety percent covered. The bounded fraction rises with the content, for $\ke$ from \resResZeroKeBounded\% at none
to \resResTwentyKeBounded\% at $20\%$ and for $\ka$ from \resResZeroKaBounded\% to \resResTwentyKaBounded\%
(Appendix~\ref{apx:phase13}). Breaking the symmetry helps in proportion to the residue, but a tenth of the meal does not make
the rates identifiable from these data: Proposition~\ref{prop:breaking} is necessary for a bounded interval and, at this noise, not
sufficient. The residue is a design choice for a control and not a model of free-living meals.

\subsection{A second model class}
\label{sec:dm}

The Dalla Man meal model \citep{dallaman2007}, in our implementation, has a three-compartment gut with a nonlinear emptying law
and so inherits no exact exchange symmetry. Fitted to the same iAUC data of the same \resDmN{} subjects, its Fisher information
has median effective rank \resDmRankMedian{} of six parameters, the profile interval is bounded for $k_{abs}$, $k_{max}$ and
$k_{min}$ in \resDmBoundedKabs\%, \resDmBoundedKmax\% and \resDmBoundedKmin\% of subjects, and the estimates sit on a bound of
their range in \resDmPinnedKabs, \resDmPinnedKmax{} and \resDmPinnedKmin{} subjects. Under the iAUC target, in two models that
differ in the structure of the gut, the gut parameters are not recovered. We ran no replica for this model, so we cannot say
how much of its non-identification belongs to the target and how much to misfit.
