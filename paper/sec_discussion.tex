\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Implications for mechanistic digital twins.}
Applying the three evidence levels from Section~\ref{sec:intro} clarifies what our experiments do and do not establish.
Under $180$\,min iAUC, even likelihood boundedness fails for individual gut rates while held-out iAUC stays near the oracle floor under maximally wrong corner rates once $\SI$ is refitted; $\SI$ itself is box-dependent and identifies a lumped gain $\gamma p_3 \SI$ conditional on fixed population constants.
Under the full trace, likelihood boundedness succeeds for $(\tau_1, p)$ even strictly inside the exact original rate domain $\mathcal{D}$, yet simulation calibration holds only when both the simulator and carbohydrate inputs are exact: carbohydrate logging error inflates boundedness while breaking coverage, and structural mismatch in the Dalla Man $\to$ Bergman control yields tightly bounded, rank-preserving intervals with zero absolute coverage.
Real physiological identification from free-living CGM logs is therefore not established by predictive accuracy or profile boundedness alone.
Because coordinate fits frequently land on the tied boundary (\resBoundaryCoords\% of iAUC folds) and the tied-rate model matches three-parameter iAUC prediction (Appendix~\ref{apx:more}), reparameterizing free-living twins around $(\SI,\tau_1)$ and communicating relative rather than absolute timing parameters is consistent with what these data support.

\paragraph{Practical recommendations.}
First, report profile-likelihood intervals (bounded, one-sided, or flat) strictly inside the feasible parameter domain across multiple box widths, alongside both interior and overall counts.
Second, audit identifiability diagnostics on both true-model replicas with realistic input error and a structurally misspecified generator: attenuation bias or model mismatch can move estimates off a box wall and inflate boundedness while breaking absolute coverage.
Third, when meal-level input error is suspected, test variance adjustments clustered by meal or explicit errors-in-variables / latent-carbohydrate likelihoods rather than trusting unadjusted profile cutoffs.
Finally, verify first-order optimizers against multi-start quasi-Newton solvers and audit clinical correlations against baseline-glucose confounding (Appendix~\ref{apx:leakage}).

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Model scope and fixed constants.}
All identifiability conclusions are conditional on our simulator equations and fixed population constants ($\gamma, p_3, p_1, p_2, n$); body weight $W$ is populated from each subject's recorded weight (Appendix~\ref{app:spec}), whereas basal glucose is fixed at $90$\,mg\,dL$^{-1}$ and sensor lag is omitted in Bergman (unlike our Dalla Man setup).
Dalla Man was fitted to real data under iAUC only (without a Dalla Man-on-Dalla-Man replica), the three cohorts are modest in size, CGMacros windows end at $180$\,min, and heavy boundary pinning in Shanghai and Hall limits interior-fraction precision.

\paragraph{Replica assumptions and input error.}
True-model replica truths derive from regularized real-data fits near the lower bound of $(\tau_1,p)$, amplifying the one-sided-to-bounded transition under upward attenuation bias and providing conditional numerical validation rather than external physiological ground truth; AR($1$) noise estimated from real residuals absorbs some misfit; and $\mathrm{CV}=0.25$ is an assumed lognormal error rather than a measured one.
Latent-carbohydrate hierarchical models remain future work.

\paragraph{Meal isolation and asymmetric boxes.}
Consecutive CGMacros meals have a median gap of \resOverlapMedianGap{} hours (\resOverlapWithinThree\% under $3$ hours; Appendix~\ref{apx:phase13}), so empty-gut isolation is approximate and a continuous multi-meal replica was not run.
Asymmetric boxes for $(\ke,\ka)$ place some swap partners outside the box (swap partners lie inside for \resBoxRealKeInside{} of \resBoxRealKeBounded{} bounded real-data trace $\ke$ intervals and \resBoxRealKaInside{} of \resBoxRealKaBounded{} for $\ka$), though our exact-$\mathcal{D}$ profiles confirm that full-trace boundedness and the coverage collapse persist without infeasible hull points (Table~\ref{tab:feasible} in Appendix~\ref{apx:numerics}).

\subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Across three CGM cohorts, true-model replicas, random-truth simulations, and a Dalla Man $\to$ Bergman misspecified-generator control, a Bergman-type digital twin fitted to postprandial iAUC stays near the oracle predictive floor even under maximally wrong gut rates while leaving $(\ke,\ka)$ completely unbounded and $\SI$ box-dependent.
Full-trace fitting yields high observed likelihood boundedness for $(\tau_1,p)$ even strictly inside the exact original rate domain $\mathcal{D}$, but absolute calibration collapses under either carbohydrate logging error (only partially repaired by meal-clustered sandwich scaling) or structural model misspecification.
Mechanistic digital twins surfacing physiological parameters should therefore pair predictive validation with exact-domain profile checks audited under both input error and model mismatch; whether errors-in-variables models or contrasting meal protocols can achieve calibrated free-living recovery remains open.

\subsubsection*{Broader Impact Statement}
The simulator evaluated here is a research model and is not validated for clinical use.
Our results highlight a deployment risk: strong predictive accuracy on summary metrics can encourage users or clinicians to interpret unidentifiable or biased twin parameters as physiological measurements, potentially distorting dietary or dosing decisions.
All analyses use public, de-identified datasets, and no new human data were collected.
