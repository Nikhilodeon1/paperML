\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results say about twins.}
Held-out iAUC error could not tell a determined parameter from an undetermined one. The iAUC carries little
information about the gut rates, the data bound insulin sensitivity for a minority of subjects even when the model is
true, and whether they determine a combination of the rates is not established (Sections~\ref{sec:timing},
\ref{sec:si} and \ref{sec:more}); none of this changes how well the fitted model predicts the held-out area
(Section~\ref{sec:prediction}). A model whose $\ke$ and $\ka$ are held at population values predicts the held-out
area about as well as one whose values were fitted, within the margin and with a small advantage for the fitted values
(H8, and H20 in a second cohort), as Proposition~\ref{prop:moments} leads one to expect of an area. That statement is
about the target. Held-out peak time and trace error are worse with the fitted timing parameters, so a check on a
different target would register a cost that the iAUC check hides. A twin that shows a person a fitted gut rate or
insulin sensitivity should therefore carry an identifiability analysis, and the analysis needs controls of its own.

\paragraph{Recommendations for fitting a twin to free-living data.}
Report profile intervals rather than point estimates, classify each as bounded, one-sided or flat, and report it at
more than one parameter range, because a one-sided interval is the prior range speaking. Define the set of subjects
over which a fraction is taken: parameters that are not identified sit on bounds, so a definition that excludes every
subject with a parameter on a bound selects an empty set. Run a replica in which the model is true, give it the input
error you expect, here the carbohydrate log, and check the interval cutoff there. In our replica an input error made
trace intervals more often bounded and less often right, and a real-data result that exceeds the replica should not
be taken at face value. Check the optimizer against a better-conditioned one, because a fixed-step estimate that is
not an optimum produces profile minima below the estimate, as it did for \resPolishNotWithinTrace\% of our trace
fits. Treat clinical correlations of a fitted parameter with care: the correlation we examined survives only until the
fasting level is partialled out (Appendix~\ref{apx:leakage}).

\paragraph{Design.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:moments} says what a target must carry to identify the timing parameters: the first moment for
$\tau_1$ and the second for $p$. An area over a long window carries neither, and a short window carries some timing at
the price of information about $\SI$. The full trace carries both and its Fisher information about timing is far
larger, but our controls do not let us say that it identifies the combination. The proposition also suggests, though
we did not test it, that an intervention changing the gastric step without changing absorption, such as a liquid meal
beside a solid one, would break the exchange symmetry by design rather than by assumption.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{One model family and one target.}
Everything rests on a Bergman-type model with a two-compartment gut and the iAUC, with two richer targets for the
combination. The exchange symmetry holds for any such cascade, but the second gut model was fitted to iAUC only, on
CGMacros, and has no replica. A replica for it would show how much of its non-identification belongs to the target.

\paragraph{Cohorts.}
The cohorts are small (\resCohortCgmSubjects, \resCohortShanghaiSubjects{} and \resCohortHallSubjects{} subjects) and
differ in sensor, window and carbohydrate source. Prediction results come from two of them, and the replica and
synthetic studies use CGMacros meal schedules. In the other two cohorts most estimates sit on a bound at the primary
box, which limits what interior fractions can say. A larger free-living cohort with weighed meals would settle it.

\paragraph{The replica.}
Its truths are fitted values near the upper bound of the timing rates, its noise is estimated from the residuals of
the real fit and so includes misfit, and its carbohydrate error is lognormal with a fixed coefficient of variation of
$0.25$. The synthetic study removes the first limitation only; H24 and H25 vary the second and the third on one seed.

\paragraph{Noise models.}
We use one variance per subject and observable, frozen at a pilot fit, with first-order autocorrelation for the trace.
Two heteroskedastic variants for the iAUC left the result for $\SI$ unchanged. We did not test heteroskedastic noise
for the trace or non-Gaussian noise.

\paragraph{The interval criterion.}
A rise of $\delta$ is an asymptotic criterion, and with a few tens of meals per subject and a hard box it can
undercover. Under the trace with the logging error the nominal interval covered the truth for \resReplicaCoordsCoverTauTrace\% and
\resReplicaCoordsCoverPTrace\% of bounded intervals, coverage fell further as the replica noise fell, and it was
adequate (\resExactOneCoverTau\% and \resExactOneCoverP\%) only with exact carbohydrate at the estimated noise. The
optimizer misses the optimum for \resPolishNotWithinTrace\% of trace fits, and we did not profile from a global
optimizer; a global search is what would test the second source of undercoverage at low noise.

\paragraph{Isolated meals and fixed parameters.}
Every meal starts with an empty gut, and each meal is simulated alone. Residual content breaks the symmetry in
proportion to its size (Proposition~\ref{prop:breaking}). A day-continuous replica, in which a snack or a long previous
meal leaves a residue, was not run, and a protocol with fasting periods before each meal would measure the residue.
Two parameters of the glucose equations, $\gamma$ and $p_3$, are fixed at population values, and a perturbation of them
was not run; a fixed value that is wrong would be absorbed by the fitted ones.

\paragraph{Post hoc hypotheses.}
H13 to H26 were declared after earlier results were seen. Their thresholds were fixed before each run but chosen
knowing the direction of the earlier results. We report all of them. Of the \resPlannedGraded{} planned hypotheses with
a pass-or-fail rule, \resPlannedMet{} were met; of the \resLaterGraded{} later ones, \resLaterMet{} were met. Of
the primary endpoints, which are planned, \resPrimaryNotMet{} were not met. Appendix~\ref{app:register} marks each
hypothesis as planned or post hoc.

\paragraph{Not run.}
A design sweep over meal size and timing, and a replica or synthetic study for the second gut model, were not run
(Appendix~\ref{app:notrun}).

\subsection{Conclusion}

A twin fitted to the iAUC of postprandial CGM can predict the held-out area about as well as any fit we tried while the
data leave its gut rates undetermined and bound its insulin sensitivity for a minority of people; a good fit is not a
measurement. We recommend that any twin whose parameters are shown to a person ship with a profile-likelihood analysis
that has been checked on a replica carrying the input error to be expected. The open question is whether a richer
target, or a protocol that separates gastric emptying from absorption, can make the gut rates identifiable.
