\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results say about twins.}
Held-out iAUC error could not tell a determined parameter from an undetermined one: on replicas with random truths the fit's
held-out error is \resPvpRandomZeroRatio{} (seed zero) and \resPvpRandomOneRatio{} (seed one) times that of the true model while
the median root mean squared log error of its rates is \resPvpRandomZeroTiming{} and \resPvpRandomOneTiming{}. The iAUC carries
little information about the gut rates and bounds insulin sensitivity for a minority of subjects even when the model is true,
yet the fitted model predicts the held-out area as well as any fit we tried, as Proposition~\ref{prop:moments} leads one to
expect of an area. Held-out peak time and trace error do register the cost of the fitted timing parameters. A twin that shows a
person a fitted gut rate or insulin sensitivity should therefore carry an identifiability analysis, and the analysis needs
controls of its own.

\paragraph{Recommendations.}
Report profile intervals rather than point estimates, classify each as bounded, one-sided or flat, and report it at more than
one parameter range, because a one-sided interval is the prior range speaking. State the set of subjects over which a fraction
is taken and give the overall count beside the interior one. Run a replica in which the model is true, give it the input error
you expect, here the carbohydrate log, and check the interval cutoff there: a real-data result that exceeds the replica should
not be taken at face value. Check the optimizer against a better-conditioned one, and treat clinical correlations of a fitted
parameter with care (Appendix~\ref{apx:leakage}).

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Model and target.}
Everything rests on a Bergman-type model with a two-compartment gut, the iAUC and the trace. The exchange symmetry holds for any
such cascade, but the second gut model was fitted to iAUC only and has no replica. Basal glucose is fixed at a population value
and the sensor is treated as an unlagged reading of plasma glucose, so a mismatch in either would enter the residual as misfit.

\paragraph{Cohorts.}
The cohorts are small and differ in sensor, window and carbohydrate source. Prediction results come from two of them, the replica
and synthetic studies use CGMacros meal schedules, and in the other two cohorts most estimates sit on a bound, which limits
what interior fractions can say.

\paragraph{The replica and the interval.}
The replica's truths are fitted values near the upper bound of the timing rates, its noise includes misfit, and its logging error
is lognormal. The carbohydrate-error sweep replicates on three seeds and on Shanghai; the grid and solver checks ran on one seed
and one cohort. A rise of $\delta$ in the likelihood is an asymptotic criterion, and the likelihood treats the logged carbohydrate
as exact, so its intervals are too narrow when the log is not. A sandwich or bootstrap interval is the obvious repair and we did
not test it, so on real data, where the input error is unknown, a nominal interval for the combination cannot be taken as
calibrated. Noise is one variance per subject and observable with first-order autocorrelation for the trace; other noise models
were not tested for the trace.

\paragraph{Isolated meals, fixed parameters and boxes.}
Every meal starts with an empty gut, but consecutive logged meals are close (median gap \resOverlapMedianGap{} hours,
\resOverlapWithinThree\% under three hours; Appendix~\ref{apx:phase13}), and residual content breaks the symmetry in proportion to
its size (Proposition~\ref{prop:breaking}). A day-continuous replica was not run. The fixed $\gamma$ and $p_3$ are absorbed by
$\SI$ when wrong (Section~\ref{sec:si}). The boxes for $\ke$ and $\ka$ differ, so the swap partner of an estimate can lie outside
them; for the bounded real-data trace intervals in rate coordinates it lies inside for \resBoxRealKeInside{} of
\resBoxRealKeBounded{} ($\ke$) and \resBoxRealKaInside{} of \resBoxRealKaBounded{} ($\ka$), and we did not recompute the tables with
a common box.

\subsection{Conclusion}

A twin fitted to the iAUC of postprandial CGM can predict the held-out area about as well as any fit we tried while the data
leave its gut rates undetermined and bound its insulin sensitivity for a minority of people; a good fit is not a measurement.
We recommend that any twin whose parameters are shown to a person ship with a profile-likelihood analysis that has been
checked on a replica carrying the input error to be expected. The open question is whether a richer target, or a protocol that separates gastric emptying from absorption, for
example a liquid meal beside a solid one, can make the gut rates identifiable.

\subsection*{Broader impact}
The simulator is a research model and has not been validated for clinical use. The risk we document is that a twin which
predicts well can lead a user or clinician to read its parameters as physiology: a wrong insulin sensitivity could shape diet or
dosing advice, and carbohydrate logging error shifts it without any warning in the interval. The analyses use public,
de-identified cohorts and no new data were collected.
