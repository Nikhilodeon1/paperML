\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Identifiability and profile likelihood.}
Structural identifiability asks whether parameters can be recovered from noise-free outputs, and practical identifiability asks the same of finite, noisy data \citep{bellman1970structural,rothenberg1971identification,chis2011structural,wieland2021structural}.
Profile likelihood diagnoses both \citep{raue2009structural}, and combined with Fisher-information subset selection it locates identifiable parameter combinations \citep{eisenberg2014determining}.
Wide eigenvalue spectra like the one in Section~\ref{sec:timing} are the signature of sloppy models in systems biology \citep{brown2003statistical,gutenkunst2007universally}.
Symbolic tools include SIAN \citep{hong2019sian} and StructuralIdentifiability.jl \citep{dong2023differential}; we run the latter.
Recent work also studies non-identifiable hemodynamic models by neural inversion \citep{tong2025invaert}.
To these diagnostics we add simulations with known truth, which test whether a bounded interval is also a calibrated one.

\paragraph{Glucose models and personalization.}
The minimal model estimates insulin sensitivity from intravenous glucose data \citep{bergman1979,bergman1981}; for meals, see also the oral glucose minimal model \citep{dallaman2002oral}.
Bayesian estimation has been proposed as a remedy for the zero-sensitivity estimates that least squares can return in type~$2$ diabetes \citep{pillonetto2002minimal}.
Meal simulators add gastrointestinal dynamics with several compartments \citep{dallaman2007,dallaman2014uva,cobelli2009diabetes}.
\citet{erdos2024leveraging} examine practical identifiability during an oral glucose tolerance test with plasma insulin; we have free-living meals and no insulin.
\citet{garciatirado2018identifiability} study the structural and practical identifiability of three control-oriented models for type~$1$ diabetes, using CGM, insulin pump, and meal records from five patients; we have no insulin records, and we check intervals against known truth.
For data assimilation applied to personalized glucose forecasting, see \citet{albers2017personalized}.

\paragraph{Input error and misspecification.}
In our setting the carbohydrate content of a meal is an input that the user estimates; on the accuracy of carbohydrate counting, see \citet{brazeau2013carbohydrate}.
For a measurement-error remedy, simulation-extrapolation, see \citet{cook1994simulation}, and for maximum likelihood under a misspecified model, see \citet{white1982maximum}.
In simulation-based inference \citep{greenberg2019automatic,tejerocantero2020sbi}, recent work studies and detects gaps between simulator and data \citep{ward2022robust,schmitt2023detecting}, and simulation-based calibration checks the validity of posterior computations \citep{talts2018sbc}.
Our replicas play a similar role for frequentist profile intervals.

\paragraph{Digital twins and glucose prediction.}
\citet{hoang2025t1dsbi} apply neural posterior estimation to a type~$1$ diabetes twin, and \citet{kuang2024medreal2sim} infer the parameters of a medical twin from noninvasive data with physics-informed self-supervised learning.
Data-driven models predict postprandial responses from meals and subject features \citep{zeevi2015personalized,berry2020human}, and GlucoBench collects CGM forecasting benchmarks \citep{sergazinov2024glucobench}.
Our question applies where a model's parameters are reported as quantities about a person: whether the data determine them, and whether predictive accuracy would show that they do not.
