\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Identifiability and profile likelihood.}
Structural identifiability concerns unique parameter recovery from noise-free trajectories, whereas practical identifiability concerns finite, noisy data \citep{bellman1970structural,rothenberg1971identification,chis2011structural,wieland2021structural}.
Profile likelihood diagnoses both structural and practical non-identifiability \citep{raue2009structural} and can be paired with Fisher subset selection to locate identifiable combinations \citep{eisenberg2014determining}.
The multi-decade eigenvalue spread in Section~\ref{sec:timing} mirrors sloppy parameter spectra in systems biology \citep{brown2003statistical,gutenkunst2007universally}.
For symbolic analysis, software packages include SIAN \citep{hong2019sian} and StructuralIdentifiability.jl \citep{dong2023differential}; we run the latter.
Recent work also inverts non-identifiable hemodynamic models with neural networks \citep{tong2025invaert}.
We extend these diagnostic tools with matched ground-truth controls (true-model replicas and random-truth studies) to test directly whether bounded profile intervals on real data are calibrated under input error.

\paragraph{Glucose--insulin modeling and CGM prediction.}
The minimal model estimates insulin sensitivity from intravenous glucose decay \citep{bergman1979,bergman1981}, where nonlinear least squares frequently returns zero $\SI$ in type~$2$ diabetes \citep{pillonetto2002minimal}.
Meal simulators add multi-compartment gastrointestinal dynamics \citep{dallaman2007,dallaman2014uva,cobelli2009diabetes}.
\citet{erdos2024leveraging} evaluated practical identifiability of personalized models calibrated on clinical oral glucose tolerance tests with CGM and plasma insulin, finding improved identifiability with CGM relative to venous samples.
We instead evaluate free-living self-logged meals without plasma insulin under iAUC and full-trace targets.
Data-driven models predict postprandial responses from meal logs and subject features \citep{zeevi2015personalized,berry2020human}, and GlucoBench standardizes CGM forecasting datasets \citep{sergazinov2024glucobench}; because they do not infer physiological parameters, parameter identifiability is specific to mechanistic digital twins.

\paragraph{Differentiable simulation, SBI, and digital twins.}
Differentiable ODE solvers in JAX and Diffrax \citep{bradbury2018jax,kidger2021diffrax} support gradient-based biophysical calibration \citep{jaxley2025}, alongside probabilistic ODE filtering \citep{kersting2020differentiable}, diffusion tempering for ODE problems with many local minima \citep{beck2024diffusion}, and neural-operator surrogates \citep{liu2026ino}.
In simulation-based inference (SBI), neural density estimators learn amortized posteriors \citep{greenberg2019automatic,tejerocantero2020sbi}, recent work diagnoses simulation-to-reality misspecification \citep{ward2022robust,schmitt2023detecting}, and simulation-based calibration checks posterior validity \citep{talts2018sbc}.
Our replica plays an analogous validation role for frequentist profile intervals.
For diabetes and medical digital twins, \citet{hoang2025t1dsbi} apply neural posterior estimation, \citet{tran2026physioseq2seq} pair matched physiological twins with sequence models, \citet{li2026glucofm} pretrain a CGM foundation model, \citet{kuang2024medreal2sim} infer twin parameters via physics-informed self-supervised learning, and \citet{pan2026hipno} quotient out scale symmetries in hemodynamic operators.
Our focus is complementary: auditing whether free-living postprandial CGM data identify individual parameters of a fitted mechanistic twin and whether held-out prediction exposes non-identifiability.
