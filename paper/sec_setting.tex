\section{Setting: model, observations, estimators and instruments}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{The simulator}
\label{sec:model}

The simulator is a Bergman-type glucose--insulin model written in JAX, integrated with the adaptive Tsit5 solver
of Diffrax in double precision, with a two-compartment gut. After a meal at time $0$, with plasma glucose $G$,
plasma insulin $I$, remote insulin action $X$, and stomach and intestinal glucose $Q_1$ and $Q_2$,
\begin{align}
\dot Q_1 &= -\ke\, Q_1 + m(t), &
\dot Q_2 &= \ke\, Q_1 - \ka\, Q_2, \label{eq:gut}\\
\dot G &= -(p_1 + X)\,G + p_1 G_b + \frac{\ka\, Q_2}{V_g}, &
\dot I &= -n\,(I - I_b) + \gamma\,[G - G_b]_+, \label{eq:glucose}\\
\dot X &= -p_2 X + p_3\, \SI\,(I - I_b), \label{eq:action}
\end{align}
where $m(t)$ is a Gaussian pulse (width $2$\,min) delivering the glucose mass of the meal,
$V_g=1.6\,W$ dL for body weight $W$, and $G_b$, $I_b$ are basal values. A softplus smooths the positive part.
The full engine carries further slow substrate states (glycogen, ketones, energy expenditure), but in the
postprandial window the glucose dynamics are carried by the five states of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:gut}--\eqref{eq:action}. Appendix~\ref{app:spec} gives the specification with every constant and
the vector field.

Each person has three free parameters, $\theta=(\SI,\ke,\ka)$, each confined to a box
(Table~\ref{tab:bounds}); everything else is fixed at population values. The \emph{primary box} is the one fixed
in the analysis plan. We also report a box half as wide and one twice as wide, scaled in logarithms about the
centre of each interval. The gut starts empty at each meal, a convention that matters for the symmetry in
Section~\ref{sec:theory}.

\subsection{Observables}
\label{sec:observables}

A \emph{meal record} is a window of CGM samples around one meal, with the logged carbohydrate, fat and fibre.
All observables are computed from the baseline-subtracted excursion $y(t)=[G(t)-\bar G_{\mathrm{pre}}]_+$, where
$\bar G_{\mathrm{pre}}$ is the mean of the samples in the $30$ minutes before the meal.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{iAUC}: $\int y\,dt$ over the post-meal window, one number per meal. It is the target of the primary
  analysis.
\item \emph{Centroid}: $\int t\,y\,dt \,/ \int y\,dt$, the time centre of mass of the excursion. It carries
  timing and almost no magnitude.
\item \emph{Trace}: the baseline-subtracted curve itself, at the sampling resolution of the cohort.
\end{itemize}
The three form a \emph{ladder}: iAUC, iAUC with centroid, full trace. Each has a smooth form for gradients
(softplus and softmax of sharpness $10$) and a hard form for scoring; on real excursions the two agree to within
one percent of the hard value.

\subsection{Likelihood, estimation and the identifiability criterion}
\label{sec:estimation}

Every identifiability analysis uses the unregularized maximum-likelihood estimate, never the penalized
prediction fit, because a penalty toward the population values would narrow every interval. Residuals are
Gaussian with a per-subject, per-observable variance estimated at a pilot fit and then held fixed; stacked
observables are weighted by their inverse residual variance; and the trace is whitened for first-order
autocorrelation (Prais--Winsten). The variances stay frozen because re-estimating them inside the optimization
would let the fit lower its loss by declaring its errors noise. Estimates come from fixed-step Adam
\citep{kingma2015adam} in log coordinates; Section~\ref{sec:more} replaces them by L-BFGS-B estimates
\citep{byrd1995limited} for the coordinate fits.

\begin{definition}[Profile interval and its classes]
\label{def:profile}
With negative log-likelihood $\NLL(\theta)$, the $95\%$ profile interval for $\theta_j$ is the set where
$\min_{\theta_{-j}}\NLL(\theta_j,\theta_{-j})-\min_\theta \NLL(\theta) \le \delta$, with
$\delta=\resDeltaThreshold$, half the $0.95$ quantile of $\chi^2_1$ (on profile-likelihood intervals see
\citealp{venzon1988method}). The profile is
\emph{bounded} if it rises above $\delta$ on both sides inside the box, \emph{one-sided} if on one side only,
and \emph{flat} if it never reaches $\delta$ inside the box.
\end{definition}

Only a bounded interval is called identified. A one-sided interval is constrained on its open side by the box,
that is, by the prior range, which is why every result is also reported at three box widths. For the two rates
the grid is also extended to four times the upper bound as a diagnostic outside the classification.

\begin{definition}[Interior subject]
\label{def:interior}
Subject $i$ is \emph{interior for parameter $j$} when the estimate lies more than $1\%$ of the box width from both
bounds.
\end{definition}

A parameter's interval can be bounded only if its estimate is off the bounds, so we report fractions of bounded
intervals over all subjects and over subjects interior for the parameter. The plan first defined interior as ``no
parameter on a bound'', which gives an empty set whenever one parameter is unidentified; Amendment two
(Appendix~\ref{app:register}) replaced it.

\subsection{Instruments}
\label{sec:instruments}

\paragraph{Fisher information.}
We compute $J=\partial(\text{weighted predictions})/\partial\log\theta$ by reverse-mode automatic
differentiation, $F=J^\top J$, its eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and the Schur complement of the timing block
$\{\ke,\ka\}$ after profiling out $\SI$, whose condition number measures how well the data constrain the timing
parameters jointly. The \emph{exchange direction} is $(1/\ka,\,-1/\ke)$ in $(\log\ke,\log\ka)$, the direction
along which $\tau_1=1/\ke+1/\ka$ is constant to first order. We report the absolute cosine between it and the
weakest eigenvector of the timing block.

\paragraph{Generic rank.}
The rank of the stacked sensitivity matrix over many random parameter draws, at a relative tolerance of
$10^{-6}$, separates a parameter set that is locally identifiable but ill-conditioned from one that is rank
deficient.

\paragraph{Structural identifiability.}
Symbolic analysis with StructuralIdentifiability.jl \citep{dong2023differential}, under several formulations that
differ in what is assumed known about the gut at the start of a meal (Section~\ref{sec:timing}).

\paragraph{Replica and synthetic study.}
In the \emph{replica}, each subject's regularized prediction-fit estimate, moved at least $5\%$ of the box width
inside the box, is the truth. Each real meal is simulated at it; autoregressive CGM noise with the subject's own
variance and lag-one correlation, taken from the residual of the real fit, is added; and the fit receives the true
carbohydrate multiplied by lognormal error of unit mean and coefficient of variation $0.25$. The replica asks how
often the data would bound a parameter if the model were true, with this noise and this logging error. In the
\emph{synthetic study} the true parameters are instead drawn independently and uniformly inside the box, so that
recovery can be read against where the truth lies. In both, coverage of the bounded intervals can be checked.
Random streams are seeded from a hash of the subject, the replica seed and the stream name, so switching one noise
source off leaves the others' draws unchanged.

\paragraph{Optimizer check.}
For the rate-space fits we compare the Adam estimate with L-BFGS-B from five random starting points and read the
gap in negative log-likelihood against $\delta$. Section~\ref{sec:more} uses the L-BFGS-B estimate itself for the
coordinate fits.

\paragraph{Data, protocol and statistics.}
We use CGMacros \citep{das2025cgmacros}, a Shanghai cohort of people with type two diabetes \citep{zhao2023shanghai} and
a cohort with standardized meals \citep{hall2018glucotypes}, keeping subjects with at least ten meals (five for the
last): \resCohortCgmSubjects, \resCohortShanghaiSubjects{} and \resCohortHallSubjects{} subjects. Held-out
prediction uses five repeats of five-fold cross-validation within subject, with SHA-256-seeded folds identical for
every method. Two methods are called equivalent when the $90\%$ bootstrap interval of their paired difference lies
inside $(-150,+150)$\,mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min, by the two one-sided tests procedure \citep{schuirmann1987comparison}.
Fractions carry Wilson intervals \citep{wilson1927probable}, paired differences carry subject-level BCa bootstrap
intervals \citep{efron1987better}, and families of comparisons carry Holm correction \citep{holm1979simple}.
Hypotheses H1--H10 were fixed in version control before the analyses, H11--H12 were declared before their
analyses, and H13--H28 were declared after earlier results had been seen; the last group is marked post hoc
wherever it appears. Appendices~\ref{app:setting} and~\ref{app:register} give the cohorts, the protocol and every
hypothesis.
