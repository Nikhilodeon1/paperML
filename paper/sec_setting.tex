\section{Setting: model, observations, estimators, and instruments}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{The simulator}
\label{sec:model}

Our primary simulator is a Bergman-type glucose--insulin ODE model implemented in JAX and integrated in double precision with Diffrax's adaptive Tsit5 solver, coupled to a two-compartment gut.
After a meal at time $0$, plasma glucose $G$, plasma insulin $I$, remote insulin action $X$, stomach glucose $Q_1$, and intestinal glucose $Q_2$ evolve as
\begin{align}
\dot Q_1 &= -\ke\, Q_1 + m(t), &
\dot Q_2 &= \ke\, Q_1 - \ka\, Q_2, \label{eq:gut}\\
\dot G &= -(p_1 + X)\,G + p_1 G_b + \frac{\ka\, Q_2}{V_g}, &
\dot I &= -n\,(I - I_b) + \gamma\,[G - G_b]_+, \label{eq:glucose}\\
\dot X &= -p_2 X + p_3\, \SI\,(I - I_b), \label{eq:action}
\end{align}
where $m(t)$ is a Gaussian rate pulse (width $2$\,min) delivering the meal's glucose mass, $V_g=1.6\,W$\,dL for each subject's recorded body weight $W$, and $G_b$ and $I_b$ are basal glucose and insulin levels.
A softplus smooths $[\cdot]_+$.
Appendix~\ref{app:spec} details the full vector field, fixed constants, and solver tolerances.

Each subject has three free parameters, $\theta=(\SI,\ke,\ka)$, constrained to a box (Table~\ref{tab:bounds}); all remaining physiological constants are fixed at population values (so all identifiability conclusions are conditional on these fixed constants and subject biometrics).
Alongside the \emph{primary box} ($1\times$), we evaluate a half-width ($0.5\times$) and double-width ($2\times$) box, scaled logarithmically about each interval's center.
Every meal simulation initializes an empty gut ($Q_1(0)=Q_2(0)=0$).

\subsection{Observables}
\label{sec:observables}

A \emph{meal record} comprises a CGM window around one meal plus logged carbohydrate, fat, and fiber.
Observables are computed from the baseline-subtracted excursion $y(t)=[G(t)-\bar G_{\mathrm{pre}}]_+$, where $\bar G_{\mathrm{pre}}$ is the mean glucose in the $30$\,min before the meal:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{iAUC}: $\int_0^T y\,dt$ over the post-meal window ($T=180$\,min in CGMacros and Shanghai; $145$\,min in the standardized-meal cohort; Table~\ref{tab:cohorts} in Appendix~\ref{app:setting}).
\item \emph{Centroid}: $\int_0^T t\,y\,dt \,/ \int_0^T y\,dt$, the temporal center of mass of the excursion.
\item \emph{Trace}: the baseline-subtracted curve at the cohort's native sampling resolution.
\end{itemize}
These form an observable \emph{ladder} (iAUC, iAUC with centroid, full trace).
Smooth versions (softplus/softmax, sharpness $10$) supply gradients and agree with hard evaluation forms within $1\%$ on real excursions.

\subsection{Likelihood, estimation, and identifiability criteria}
\label{sec:estimation}

Identifiability analyses use unregularized maximum likelihood ($\lambda=0$) with the Gaussian noise model below.
The prediction fit of Section~\ref{sec:prediction} minimizes the mean squared iAUC error with a penalty of weight $0.01$ on the squared, box-normalized distance to the population value.
That error is of order $\resPenaltyLossScale$ (mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min)$^2$ at the fit, because the mean absolute error is \resMaeGradThree{} mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min, whereas the penalty cannot exceed $0.03$.
The penalty therefore acts only along directions in which the data are flat, and the prediction fit, and the replica truths taken from it, are in practice unregularized least-squares fits of the iAUC.
Residuals are Gaussian with per-subject, per-observable variances estimated from a pilot fit and then frozen (preventing variance inflation inside optimization); stacked observables use inverse-variance weights, and trace residuals are Prais--Winsten whitened for lag-$1$ autocorrelation.
Fits use fixed-step Adam \citep{kingma2015adam} in log coordinates, and the estimates of the profile analyses are then refined from several starting points with L-BFGS-B \citep{byrd1995limited}.

\begin{definition}[Profile interval and classification]
\label{def:profile}
For negative log-likelihood $\NLL(\theta)$, the $95\%$ profile interval for $\theta_j$ is $\{\theta_j : \min_{\theta_{-j}}\NLL(\theta_j,\theta_{-j})-\min_\theta \NLL(\theta) \le \delta\}$, with $\delta=\resDeltaThreshold$ (half the $0.95$ quantile of $\chi^2_1$; \citealp{venzon1988method}).
A profile is \emph{bounded} if it exceeds $\delta$ on both sides of the optimum within the box, \emph{one-sided} if on one side only, and \emph{flat} if it stays below $\delta$ across the box.
\end{definition}

A bounded interval is necessary for practical identifiability, not sufficient: it says that the likelihood closes inside the box, not that the estimate is accurate. We use \emph{identified} only for a parameter whose bounded interval also covers the truth in a simulation, and say \emph{bounded} otherwise.
For $\ke$ and $\ka$ we also report the primary box, a box twice as wide, and a box four times as wide, because a bounded interval cannot be found for an estimate on the wall of a narrow box.

\begin{definition}[Interior subject]
\label{def:interior}
Subject $i$ is \emph{interior for parameter $j$} when $\hat\theta_{i,j}$ lies more than $1\%$ of the box width from both bounds.
\end{definition}

Boundary-pinned estimates cannot yield bounded profiles, so we report bounded counts both among interior subjects and over all subjects, and we do not read a fraction from fewer than $10$ interior subjects.

\subsection{Diagnostic instruments and data}
\label{sec:instruments}

\paragraph{Fisher information, generic rank, and symbolic structure.}
We compute $J=\partial(\text{weighted predictions})/\partial\log\theta$ by reverse-mode autodiff, form $F=J^\top J$, and examine the Schur complement of the timing block $\{\ke,\ka\}$ after profiling out $\SI$.
The \emph{exchange tangent} $(1/\ka,\,-1/\ke)$ in $(\log\ke,\log\ka)$ is the direction along which $\tau_1=1/\ke+1/\ka$ is locally constant; we measure its absolute cosine with the weakest timing-block eigenvector.
Generic numerical rank of $J$ across random draws (tolerance $10^{-6}$) separates ill-conditioning from rank deficiency, while StructuralIdentifiability.jl \citep{dong2023differential} tests symbolic identifiability under alternative initial-gut assumptions.

\paragraph{Replica and synthetic controls.}
In the \emph{true-model replica}, each subject's prediction-fit estimate (moved at least $5\%$ inside the box) is the ground truth; real meals are simulated from that truth, corrupted with first-order autoregressive (AR($1$)) CGM noise matching the subject's empirical residual variance and lag-$1$ correlation, and fed to the estimator with lognormal carbohydrate logging error (unit mean, $\mathrm{CV}=0.25$).
In the \emph{synthetic study}, truths are drawn independently and uniformly inside the box.
The replica's fitted truths sit near the upper bound of the rates, and therefore near one edge of the range searched in the combined coordinates of Section~\ref{sec:more}, so we also draw replica truths at random through the box (\emph{random-truth replica}).
Both controls evaluate coverage conditional on their simulation assumptions and give no independent physiological validation.
\emph{Coverage} means one thing throughout: the share of all subjects whose $95\%$ profile interval, clipped to the box, contains the true value.
An interval open to the box edge covers the truth almost for free, so we also report the share of bounded intervals, the coverage of bounded intervals narrower than $0.5$ in log units, and the median interval score.

\paragraph{Cohorts, cross-validation, and statistics.}
We use CGMacros \citep{das2025cgmacros}, a Shanghai type~$2$ diabetes cohort \citep{zhao2023shanghai}, and a standardized-meal cohort \citep{hall2018glucotypes}, retaining subjects with at least $10$ meals ($5$ for the standardized-meal cohort): \resCohortCgmSubjects, \resCohortShanghaiSubjects, and \resCohortHallSubjects{} subjects.
The Shanghai carbohydrates are estimated by keyword from free-text dietary records and its sensor samples every $15$ minutes, so that cohort carries both a larger logging error and a coarser trace than CGMacros; we use it only for replication.
Held-out prediction uses $5$ repeats of $5$-fold within-subject cross-validation with identical SHA-256-seeded folds across methods.
Two fits are called equivalent when the $90\%$ bootstrap interval of their paired difference lies inside a margin (two one-sided tests; \citealp{schuirmann1987comparison}). The margin is $150$\,mg\,dL$^{-1}$\,min, which is \resMarginShareGain\% of the gain of the three-parameter fit over the personal mean, so Section~\ref{sec:prediction} also reports the differences themselves.
Proportions report $95\%$ Wilson intervals \citep{wilson1927probable}, paired differences report subject-level BCa bootstrap intervals \citep{efron1987better}, and test families use Holm correction \citep{holm1979simple}.
Ten hypotheses were fixed in version control before any analysis (Table~\ref{tab:plan}). Every other analysis was added after earlier results had been seen and is exploratory; the full record is kept with the code. Cohort and protocol details are in Appendix~\ref{app:setting}.
