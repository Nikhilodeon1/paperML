## 1. The paper in one paragraph

Digital twins fitted to wearable data are routinely read as physiology ("your insulin sensitivity is low"), and held-out prediction is the usual evidence that this is safe. We take one Bergman-type glucose-insulin simulator (three personal parameters: insulin sensitivity S_I, gastric emptying rate k_e, carbohydrate absorption rate k_a) fitted per person to one target (the baseline-subtracted incremental area under the curve, iAUC, of a meal), and ask which parameters the data determine and whether held-out prediction would reveal what they do not. The answers are: the iAUC carries almost no information about the split between k_e and k_a (an exact exchange symmetry of the empty-gut cascade, proved), S_I is bounded for only a minority of people even when the model is exactly true, and held-out iAUC prediction is the same for a fit with free timing parameters as for one with timing held at population values. A replica study in which the model is true is used to say how much of each failure belongs to the target, the noise, the optimizer or the input error.

## 4. How the instruments work (what you need to judge the evidence)

* **Model.** Bergman-type glucose-insulin model (five states in the postprandial window), two-compartment gut, JAX with Diffrax (Tsit5, double precision, default relative and absolute tolerance 1e-4). Only (S_I, k_e, k_a) are personal; everything else is fixed at population values. The gut starts empty at each meal.
* **Observables.** iAUC (one number per meal); the centroid of the excursion; the full baseline-subtracted trace. All use smooth forms (softplus, softmax, sharpness 10) for gradients and hard forms for scoring.
* **Likelihood.** Gaussian, per-subject per-observable variance estimated at a pilot fit and then frozen, inverse-variance weights, first-order autocorrelation (Prais-Winsten) for the trace. Unregularized maximum likelihood is used for every identifiability analysis.
* **Profile intervals.** The 95% interval is the set where the profile NLL is within delta = 1.92 of the minimum; "bounded" means the profile rises above delta on both sides inside the box, "one-sided" on one side, "flat" on neither. Only bounded counts as identified. Results are reported at three box widths because one-sided intervals reflect the prior range.
* **Replica.** Each subject's regularized fit, moved at least 5% of the box width inside, is the truth. Each real meal is simulated at the truth, AR(1) noise with the subject's own variance and correlation (from the real fit's residual) is added, and the fit is given the true carbohydrate times lognormal error (mean 1, coefficient of variation 0.25). The replica asks how often the data would bound a parameter if the model were true.
* **Synthetic study.** True parameters drawn uniformly inside the box, so recovery can be read against the position of the truth.
* **Cross-validation.** Five repeats of five-fold, within subject, SHA-256-seeded folds, identical for all methods. Equivalence by two one-sided tests with margin 150 mg/dL min (the margin is a stated choice).
* **Reproducibility.** Every number in the text is a LaTeX macro generated from a frozen, hashed (SHA-256) result file; a linter rejects hand-typed numbers; random streams are seeded from hashes of the unit, so results do not depend on worker count.

## 6. Limitations the paper already states (use these only to avoid duplication)

One model family and one target; small cohorts, replica and synthetic study built on CGMacros meal schedules; the replica's truths are fitted values near the upper bound of the timing rates, its noise includes misfit, its logging error is a fixed lognormal; one noise model; the interval criterion (an asymptotic delta) can undercover with few meals and a hard box; every meal is simulated alone from an empty gut (meals in the logs follow one another closely: median gap 4.8 h, 37% within 4 h); two glucose-equation parameters (gamma, p3) are fixed, and a perturbation of them is absorbed by S_I without a warning from its interval (Sec. 5.2); the positive control uses a residual gut content that is a design choice, not a liquid-and-solid design; no replica for the second model; no design sweep; the grid and solver checks use one replica seed and one cohort; the likelihood at the truth is not calibrated once the replica carries logging error, so a nominal interval for the combination cannot be taken as calibrated on real data.

## 7. Concerns the authors have and would like you to test

1. **Does the detector ever say "identified" when it should? (Answered in part, Amendments eleven to thirteen.)** For the combination (tau1, p): yes, in a replica with exact carbohydrate and a quarter of the noise, where the intervals are bounded for 80% and 71% of interior subjects and the nominal interval covers the truth for 91%, with the bounded intervals covering in 94% and 90%. For the gut rates themselves the positive control did **not** pass: with the exchange symmetry broken by a known residual gut content of 10% of the meal, k_e and k_a are bounded in 13% and 6% of interior subjects (39% and 23% at 20%), with coverage 98 to 100%. Is this the right control? Would you count 20% residual, a liquid-and-solid design or a lower noise as the appropriate pass criterion, and does the paper's reading (the structural result of Proposition 2 is necessary and, at this noise, not sufficient for a bounded interval) follow?
2. **Inner optimizer and grid (tested after the main analysis; Amendments ten, twelve and thirteen).** The fixed-step Adam inner solver, the 15-point grid and the interpolation of the profile at the truth could each overstate a profile. Results: 500 inner steps change no class (0 of 90 in each setting); a converged L-BFGS-B solver changes no class and leaves the real-data bounded fractions at 83% and 88%; a 29-point grid does the same on real data and on two replicas; the earlier low-noise undercoverage (16% and 20%) came from interpolating a narrow profile across the 15-point grid, not from the likelihood (calibrated at the truth), the optimizer (45 of 45 within the cutoff) or the solver. Two things remain open: the checks use one replica seed and one cohort, and with carbohydrate error the likelihood at the truth is not calibrated (38% above the cutoff), so real-data intervals for the combination cannot be taken as calibrated whatever the grid. Please say whether you consider the treatment honest and sufficient.
3. **Tautology risk in the prediction claim.** Proposition 3 says an area over a long window carries no timing, so "iAUC prediction does not reveal timing errors" follows by construction. The paper's answer is (i) the claim is scoped to the iAUC target, (ii) peak-time and trace prediction register a cost of the fitted timing parameters on real data, (iii) the floor-of-the-true-model measurement on replicas, and (iv) the same floor for peak time and trace, now in the paper (Amendment ten, Sec. 5.3): on the random-truth replicas the free-timing fit is 1.10 to 1.15 times its floor on those targets, and the excess rises with timing error (Spearman 0.48 and 0.52 for the peak time, 0.69 and 0.77 for the trace, intervals excluding zero), while the S_I-only fit with population timing is within 1.01 to 1.05 times the floor and uncorrelated with it. Is the scoped claim, together with this contrast, now enough?
4. **Replica realism.** The replica reuses the same simulator, so misspecification is excluded by design; the paper says it measures how often the data would bound a parameter if the model were true. Does the replica support the weight the paper puts on it? Is lognormal logging error with coefficient of variation 0.25 a reasonable central choice, given that the real-data trace fractions match a replica error of 0.5 to 0.75?
5. **Post hoc volume.** 22 of 34 hypotheses were declared after results were seen, with thresholds chosen knowing the direction. Is the planned-versus-later split reported clearly enough, and does the paper's framing of "3 of 5 primary endpoints not met" read as honest or as buried?
6. **Scope versus title.** The title says "Prediction Is Not Identification". Is it supported by one simulator, one target, three small cohorts? Would you ask for a retitle or a narrower title?
7. **Audience.** Would a TMLR reader interested in identifiability, digital twins or scientific ML take something from this beyond a cautionary example? Does the paper's contribution (the replica-with-input-error protocol and the profile-class definitions) read as general?
8. **Density.** The main results sections are heavy with numbers. Please mark anything that should move to an appendix or be cut.

## Revision after the third outside read (Amendment 15, report-only)

The manuscript was restructured: main text through the conclusion is 12 pages (it was 16); the 14 inline hypothesis codes and the chronological account of the numerical checks were removed from Sections 5 and 6 (the register, a single amendment table and one appendix subsection on numerical sensitivity carry that record); the abstract is about 260 words and states the interior and the overall counts; a Broader Impact statement was added; Section 5.4 now leads with the clean protocol and a dose-response table. New analyses (A20, `evaluation/carb_curvature.py`; `phase19_summary.py`):

* *Why does carbohydrate logging error narrow the trace intervals while coverage falls?* The reviewer's account (the error inflates the Fisher information through the second moment 1 + CV^2 of the unit-mean multiplier) was measured and **not supported**: the median squared load ratio is 1.05 at CV 0.25 and 1.33 at 0.75, but the smaller eigenvalue of the (tau1, p) information block after profiling out S_I falls from 73 (exact) to 61 (0.25) and 27 (0.75), because the noise standard deviation estimated at the fit rises from 6.03 to 6.52. What the stored profiles show instead: the median width of the bounded tau1 intervals is flat (0.15 to 0.19 in log units) while the median error of the estimate rises tenfold (0.02 to 0.19), so the error-to-half-width ratio goes from 0.5 to 2.2 (tau1) and 0.3 to 2.1 (p): the estimate moves and the interval stays. The rise of the bounded share (6 of 45 to 28 of 45 for tau1) still has no mechanism: estimates on a bound are about constant (10 to 13) and the profiles are not steeper over the box.
* *The -0.19 bias of S_I on the unperturbed replica* is -0.20 at exact carbohydrate (19 of 45 on a bound), so it is not logging error.
* Overall bounded counts of the real-data iAUC intervals: S_I 3 of 45, k_e 0, k_a 0.

A sandwich or block-bootstrap interval was not tried (listed as a limitation). The outside read's remarks on missing symbols (gamma, tau, delta) and minus signs were extraction artifacts of the PDF text (the glyphs are present in the typeset PDF); real defects fixed: an unresolved cross-reference, the unlabelled legend of Figure 1b, a duplicated scorecard table, an amendment-numbering mismatch in the register, empty appendix stubs, and the unexplained label "iauc peak".
